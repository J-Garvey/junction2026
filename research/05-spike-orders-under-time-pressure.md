# Late headcount changes, not disasters, drive spike orders

> **Research report, 14 Nov 2026.** Question: which customers place large, one-off, highly variable food orders under extreme time pressure, where speed and accuracy matter more than price, and which of them best fit an AI purchasing agent ordering from Valio Aimo? Built from five web-research strands; the cited source notes are in [notes/spike-orders/](notes/spike-orders/). Geography tags: [FI] Finland, [SE] Sweden, [UK], [US], [EU], [Global]. Where this report and [../GROUND_TRUTH.md](../GROUND_TRUTH.md) disagree, the ground truth wins. Segment ratings in the scoring table are synthesis, not sourced fact. Feeds team decision 1 in [../QUESTIONS.md](../QUESTIONS.md).

The best-fit segments for an AI purchasing agent ordering from Valio Aimo are **event and venue caterers, conference venues and non-S-Group hotels (especially Lapland in December)**. True emergencies are less suitable. These customers book events weeks ahead, but the final number and the special-diet list arrive **1–3 days before service**, close to wholesale cut-offs. A wrong or short order costs them reputation and legal risk that they cannot pass on to the client. The underlying problem is the **correction order**, not the surprise event. In Finland, Sokos Hotels fixes menus and diets 14 days out and lets numbers fall until 1–14 days before arrival. In the US, final guarantees are typically due 48–72 hours out. Actual turnout differs from registrations by a median of about 17–28%. Arena operators, festival vendors and municipal kitchens absorbing strikes or evacuations are strong second-tier fits. Cruise, ferry, airline and rail catering face hard deadlines, but they buffer volatility internally, buy through intermediaries or own logistics, or order on fixed menus. Their fit is weaker. On the supply side, no Finnish wholesaler publishes a same-day express tier, an event-order process or substitution consent. Valio Aimo's realistic rush path is the evening cut-off for 24-hour delivery or same-day click & collect in Helsinki, where out-of-stock lines are dropped without substitution. Buyers in every segment pay through buffers, surcharges, vendor-paid emergency runs and cash & carry trips to avoid shortfalls. That supports the team's premise that speed and accuracy outrank unit price. The evidence is behavioural, though: no survey or wholesaler data measures willingness to pay.

## Most spikes are booked events whose numbers move in the last 72 hours

The usual picture of a spike order is a sudden, unexpected event. Contract terms show something different in hotels, venues and event catering. The event, the menu and the price are fixed early. Quantity and diet mix are what move late. **Sokos Hotels [FI]** requires programme, names and one menu with special diets **at least 14 days before arrival**. It lets groups reduce numbers on a sliding scale: for a group under 15, 100% free until 7 days out and 10% until 1 day out. For 201–350 people the same steps fall at 84, 56, 42 and 14 days. The hotel charges no-shows in full and claims full compensation for products ordered in advance ([Sokos Hotels group terms](https://www.sokoshotels.fi/en/group-terms)). In the US, final guarantees fall **48 hours to 3 business days** before the event ([ASU Catering](https://sundevilhospitality.asu.edu/catering/asu-catering-policies-and-guidelines); [CMU catering policies 2026](https://www.cmu.edu/dining/catering/catering-policies-page-2026.pdf)). Wedding and event contracts typically lock the count 7–14 days ahead and allow **±5% until 72 hours** before ([Agiled catering contract template](https://agiled.app/contract-templates/catering)). Turnout is the real source of variance. Pheedloop's check-in data [Global] shows a **median no-show of about 28% for free events and 17% for paid ones**. For small free events it reaches about 37% ([Pheedloop Event Data Lab #05](https://pheedloop.com/blog/event-data-lab-report-5)).

The commercial result is an asymmetry. Venue contracts push the cost of over-ordering onto the client. US venues add **a 50% surcharge for same-day increases** ([CMU catering policies](https://www.cmu.edu/dining/catering/catering-policies-page-2026.pdf)). Under-supply and allergen errors stay with the venue. The best-documented failure is allergen-related. In December 2021 a best man at a UK wedding had anaphylaxis from a nut-containing canapé. The operator was fined **£16,000 plus £7,071 costs**, and the judge cited a "realistic risk of death" ([Lancaster City Council](https://www.lancaster.gov.uk/news/2023/sep/food-business-fined-after-man-suffers-a-severe-allergic-reaction-at-wedding)). A 2017 UK case involved a bride served almond-containing cake ([Trading Standards](https://www.tradingstandards.uk/news-policy/news-room/2017/catering-company-fined-after-bride-suffers-allergic-reaction-on-her-wedding-day)). No Finnish or Nordic incident data for event catering turned up.

Finland has clear seasonal peaks that sit on top of this pattern. **December pikkujoulu (Christmas-party) season** is sold as a product. Paasitorni in Helsinki lists a Christmas buffet from €59 per person (minimum 30) and a Christmas lunch on 14–18 Dec 2026 at €55 ([Paasitorni catering](https://paasitorni.fi/en/restaurants/paasiravintola/catering-for-meetings-and-banquets)). **Lapland's charter season** concentrates foreign demand in December. Statistics Finland [FI] counted 1.93 million national overnight stays in December 2025, with foreign stays (0.98 m) exceeding domestic for the first time ([Statistics Finland](https://stat.fi/fi/julkaisu/cmfzaqf1o2m9e07uq8doe39sl)). The average December Lapland room price was **€356, against €168 nationally** ([Statistics Finland](https://stat.fi/fi/uutinen/Lapin-hotellit-joulukuussa-ennaetyksellisen-kalliita-taustalla-ulkomaisten-matkailijoiden-lisaeaentyminen)). That is a market paying for reliability. The binding constraint there is thin last-mile logistics and holiday closures, more than short notice.

Arenas and festivals add a second ordering moment: **top-ups during or just before the event**, driven by sales, weather and sell-outs. At the 2023 Ice Hockey World Championships in Tampere [FI], NoHo Partners ran 22 restaurants and bars and 63 suites at Nokia Arena. It served about 2,000 VIP guests per game in a 12,000-seat hall. One outlet had sold 10,000 wings a day the year before ([Kespro, 2023](https://www.kespro.com/ideat-ja-inspiraatiot/artikkelit/mm-kiekko-kansa-syo-ja-juo-hyvin-nokia-arenalla)). Festival vendors face far wider bands. At Flow Festival [FI], Street Gastro planned against a 500-portion baseline but targeted 3,000–4,000, a **6–8× spread**. Maannos estimated 1,000–2,000. Storms once left a ceviche tent with no sales because people wanted hot food ([Yrittäjät.fi, 2019](https://www.yrittajat.fi/uutiset/festivaali-on-ravintoloitsijalle-mahdollisuus-saada-nakyvyytta-ja-muokata-imagoa-keittion-on-oltava-nopeampi-kuin-kassa/)). Sports playoffs compress the whole cycle. Levy [US] "planned, ordered, and executed in 10 days" for the 2023 World Series. It says suppliers must "alter inventory levels based on the outcome of each game" ([Levy, 2023](https://www.levyrestaurants.com/blog/2023-11-20-creating-playoff-magic-across-the-country)). Liiga playoffs and national-team matches are the likely Finnish analogue, but no Finnish evidence describes how those orders are placed.

## Transport, remote and emergency buyers face hard deadlines but absorb volatility elsewhere

The transport and remote segments have the hardest deadlines, but most of them have built ways to keep volatility out of their wholesale orders. **Cruise provisioning [US]** is the starkest case. If containers miss the 3 p.m. cut-off, "that ship is gone". Missed goods may need at-sea delivery "often at the vendor's expense". Royal Caribbean misses only 7–10 of about 5,000 containers a year, **99.96% on time**. Carnival UK runs nightly consumption-to-order forecasting, and galleys change menus when items are short ([Inbound Logistics, 2017](https://www.inboundlogistics.com/?p=14055)). Helsinki, however, is mostly a transit port. It had 101 calls and 175,000+ guests in 2025 ([Port of Helsinki](https://www.portofhelsinki.fi/en/about-us/port-of-helsinki/port-of-helsinki/whats-new/cargo-and-passenger-traffic-volumes-in-the-port-of-helsinki-remained-stable-in-2025/)), and only **15 turnaround calls** are planned for 2026 ([Port of Helsinki](https://www.portofhelsinki.fi/en/about-us/port-of-helsinki/whats-new/international-cruise-season-begins-25-march-turnaround-calls-increase/)). Ship supply also runs through chandlers and RFQ platforms such as ShipServ, where requisitions become RFQs to selected suppliers ([ShipServ](https://corporate.shipserv.com/knowledgebase/creating-reqs-rfqs-and-pos-in-shipserv-trade)). That puts a layer between a wholesaler's agent and the buyer. Baltic ferries cook in their own galleys. Viking Line sources close to 70% of ingredients from Finland, Sweden and Estonia ([Moottori.fi](https://moottori.fi/uutinen/ruoka-hyva-syy-merimatkailla-ilman-ahkya/)). Tallink supplies its fleet from its own Tallinn logistics centre ([Moodie Davitt](https://moodiedavittreport.com/?p=374013)). Ferries are frequent, deadline-bound and fairly routine.

**Flight kitchens** buffer late changes with finished-meal stock. Finnair [FI] closes special-meal requests **24 hours before departure** and will not guarantee an allergen-free cabin ([Finnair special diets](https://www.finnair.com/en/nordic-kitchen/special-diets)). Caterers over-produce because the final load is unknown ([HCLTech](https://www.hcltech.com/de-de/blogs/the-role-of-advanced-technologies-in-streamlining-operations-across-the-in-flight-catering-sector)). Helsinki has two concentrated buyers. Finnair Kitchen has about 400 staff ([Finnair](https://company.finnair.com/en/finnair-kitchen-in-a-nutshell-2781938)), and LSG Sky Chefs delivers about 8,000 meals a day ([Finavia](https://finavia.fi/sv/node/4433)). Their raw-ingredient buying is probably forecast-driven, but no source shows how they buy from wholesalers. **VR's onboard catering [FI]** (VR Ravintolapalvelut, about €36 m revenue and 295 staff) runs a fixed menu with pre-ordered group packages ([Vainu](https://search.vainu.com/company/vr-ravintolapalvelut-oy-taloustiedot-ja-liikevaihto/FI01108582/yritystiedot)). It is the weakest fit in the study.

The **remote and defence** segments are large but contract-framed. Leijona Catering [FI], the state-owned Defence Forces caterer since 2012, serves up to about **70,000–80,000 portions a day** and runs CGI Aromi ([CGI](https://cgi.com/uk/en-gb/case-study/aromi/cgi-waste-manager-reduces-food-waste-for-finnish-armed-forces); [Duunitori](https://duunitori.fi/yritys/leijona-catering)). Its tenders demand "absolute reliability, delivery certainty" and tested continuity plans from suppliers ([TED 148698-2022](https://ted.europa.eu/en/notice/148698-2022/pdf)). Peacetime ordering is regular. The spikes come from exercises, call-up cohorts and readiness raising. Industrial projects create planned step-changes. Each Olkiluoto 3 annual outage brings about 1,000 contractor workers, half from abroad ([MTV Uutiset](https://www.mtvuutiset.fi/artikkeli/olkiluoto-3-huoltoon-poissa-pelista-pari-kuukautta/9108490)). The Kittilä mine has over 1,000 people on site including contractors ([Duunitori](https://duunitori.fi/yritys/agnico-eagle-finland)). Their caterers are not publicly named.

**Emergency and surge feeding** is the most extreme case on speed, but it is episodic. In 2015 [FI] the Finnish Red Cross set up **2–4 reception units a day for 25 days**, sometimes with about four hours' notice. It peaked at 109 centres and almost 20,000 beds, with procurement centralised through its Kalkku logistics centre and standard product packages for each new unit ([Finnish Red Cross](https://www.redcross.fi/our-work/domestic/help-for-immigrants/an-exceptional-reception-operation/)). In 2022 the reception system grew from 20 centres to 77 within about five months, and a third of arrivals were children ([Migri](https://migri.fi/-/yli-35-000-hakenut-tilapaista-suojelua-suomesta-vastaanottojarjestelman-piirissa-enemman-asiakkaita-kuin-koskaan)). Migri then made **almost €900m of direct awards** on urgency grounds, against about €70m tendered over two years. That is the clearest Finnish evidence that speed overrides price in a crisis ([Verkkouutiset/Yle MOT](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/yle-vastaanottokeskuksiin-kaytettiin-lahes-900-miljoonaa-euroa-ilman-kilpailutusta/)). Even accelerated EU tenders take 10–15 days, and the urgency exemption is a narrow stop-gap ([Bevan Brittan](https://www.bevanbrittan.com/insights/articles/2020/procurement-european-commission-communication-on-public-procurement-in-the-covid-19-emergency-situation-2020c-108-101/)). In practice, surge food is therefore bought as unusual call-offs under the **existing frameworks of municipal and county central kitchens**, which favours the incumbent wholesaler. Strikes are the more frequent version. During the April 2025 food-industry strike, Espoo Catering [FI] faced "product shortages and delivery delays" and changed menus site by site ([Espoo](https://www.espoo.fi/en/impacts-spring-2025-strikes-on-espoo-caterings-operations)). Food-service wholesalers are formal members of the security-of-supply agency HVK's food-service pool (Ruokapalvelupooli) ([HVK](https://www.huoltovarmuuskeskus.fi/toimialat/elintarvikehuolto/elintarvikehuoltosektori)). A surge-capable ordering agent therefore fits a preparedness role Valio Aimo already holds.

## Kitchens still translate menus into orders by hand and use cash & carry as their express lane

Across segments, today's process is the same. A sales or catering document fixes the event. The kitchen turns it into ingredients. A chef or kitchen manager places the order, usually against a chain or group supplier agreement. Internationally the document is the **Banquet Event Order (BEO)**, which holds the guarantee, timings, menu and diets ([BoomPop](https://help.boompop.com/en/articles/11635758-what-is-a-banquet-event-order-beo-and-why-does-it-matter)). Software can scale recipes and generate per-supplier purchase orders. Apicbase [EU] supports POs from PAR levels, bills of materials or forecasts ([Apicbase](https://get.apicbase.com/production-planning/)), and Total Party Planner [US] offers recipe costing and order tracking from $65 a month ([PerfectVenue](https://www.perfectvenue.com/post/total-party-planner-review)). No vendor documentation shows event covers becoming a wholesaler order automatically, with correct pack sizes, substitutes and delivery slots. No survey measures how many kitchens use these tools rather than spreadsheets. In Finland, a head-chef job ad puts supplier relationships inside the chef role ([laura.fi](https://laura.fi/avoimet-tyopaikat/cafe-logo-oy/keittiomestari-kokki/3299468/)). NoHo's arena operations name an "operative food service manager" as the Kespro contact ([Kespro, 2023](https://www.kespro.com/ideat-ja-inspiraatiot/artikkelit/mm-kiekko-kansa-syo-ja-juo-hyvin-nokia-arenalla)). The buyer is a busy cook, not a procurement professional.

Venues reduce complexity before it reaches purchasing. They allow **one menu per group** (Sokos, Paasitorni) and set per-variety minimums, for example 20 canapés per variety ([Paasitorni](https://paasitorni.fi/en/restaurants/paasiravintola/catering-for-meetings-and-banquets)). They also build in a buffer: US caterers prepare **5% over guarantee** ([CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/dining/catering/catering-policies-page-2026.pdf)). NoHo uses "generic product choices" reusable across outlets, and it redeploys leftovers to the arena lunch service or donates them ([Kespro, 2023](https://www.kespro.com/ideat-ja-inspiraatiot/artikkelit/mm-kiekko-kansa-syo-ja-juo-hyvin-nokia-arenalla)). Leftovers are redeployed or donated rather than returned to the wholesaler. Valio's sales terms carry no obligation to take back ordered goods ([Valio Aimo myyntiehdot](https://www.valioaimo.fi/myyntiehdot/)). That pushes buyers to under-order and top up later, which makes the top-up channel critical.

The top-up channel today is **cash & carry**, with the buyer paying in staff time. The best-documented Finnish case is NoHo and Kespro at Nokia Arena. Groundwork with Kespro happened months ahead. Central-warehouse deliveries ran in restricted security windows, and cold-storage containers added capacity. For quick supply, "the roll cage waits at the loading dock, and in **18 minutes** it's at Nokia Arena" from Kespro's Nekala cash & carry ([Kespro, 2023](https://www.kespro.com/ideat-ja-inspiraatiot/artikkelit/mm-kiekko-kansa-syo-ja-juo-hyvin-nokia-arenalla)). The UK food regulator likewise notes that SME event caterers rely on "local cash and carries" as needs arise ([FSA](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-guidance/prepacked-for-direct-sale-ppds-allergen-labelling-changes-for-event-caterers)).

Substitutions and allergens are where speed and accuracy collide. A substitute SKU on an event order can carry a different allergen profile from the item on menu cards already printed for hundreds of guests. Organisers add hard constraints of their own. Flow Festival [FI] bans red meat and poultry, requires Finnish fish and a vegan dish at every restaurant ([Flow Festival](https://www.flowfestival.com/en/news/read-the-latest/flow-festivals-food-program-merges-top-restaurants-with-a-sustainable-food-selection)). Sideways at Helsinki Ice Hall was fully plant-based ([Restel/STT, 2026](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/71882959/abreun-konsertit-saavat-oman-ravintolatarjontansa-veikkaus-arenalla?publisherId=55320854)). UK wholesalers have reported poor allergen data from some suppliers and called for a central database ([The Caterer](https://thecaterer.com/all-content/food-supply-chain-allergen-natashas-law-restaurants)). Evacuation feeding adds volunteer-run kitchens and populations triaged for special needs at entry ([SPR AvunKetju2025](https://piirit.punainenristi.fi/globalassets/paikallistoiminta/avun-ketju-2025/evakuointikeskuksen-johtaminen-ja-toiminnot_avunketju2025_valmiusharjoitus-06102025.pdf)). No documented Nordic incident ties a wholesaler substitution to an allergic reaction. The risk is still structural, and every segment treats allergens as a hard constraint.

## Nordic wholesalers run scheduled routes and offer no product built for rush orders

All four major Finnish food-service wholesalers are built for recurring routes on agreed delivery days, with 2–3 orders a week and templates. **Valio Aimo [FI]** delivers on agreed delivery days from about 25,000 products in Espoo and Kuopio, in "an average of 24 hours" ([Valio Aimo](https://www.valioaimo.fi/en/become-a-customer/delivery-wholesale/); wording per [../GROUND_TRUTH.md](../GROUND_TRUTH.md)). Each product carries its own order cut-off, and no category or route cut-off times are published. Webshop customers can order stock items until the evening. Orders are picked by 21:00, six days a week. Delivery reliability is about **99%**, and peaks are handled with 100+ temporary staff on top of about 130 permanent staff. Only **8,500 SKUs sit on shelf, out of 30,000 including order-only items** ([Valio Aimo warehouse article, Sep 2025](https://www.valioaimo.fi/artikkelit/varastolla-pidetaan-tilausten-pyorat-pyorimassa/)). A large quantity of a slow-moving or order-only item is therefore the likeliest line to short-ship on an event order. It is not known whether the 99% figure is measured per delivery or per line. If it is per delivery, a 100-line event order could still lose several lines. The webshop supports baskets for several delivery dates and editing confirmed orders. The Minun Aimo portal adds recipes-to-cart and **A.I.mo**, an assistant that looks up past purchases rather than building orders ([Valio Aimo tools](https://www.valioaimo.fi/en/company/why-choose-valio-aimo-as-a-partner/innovative-tools-for-everyday-work/)). The closest rush product is **Tilaa & Nouda** click & collect at Sörnäinen, Helsinki: same- or next-day pickup from three-temperature lockers. Confirmed orders cannot be changed there, and **out-of-stock items are removed from the bill with no substitution offered** ([Tilaa & Nouda](https://www.valioaimo.fi/asiakkaaksi/pikatukut/tilaa-nouda-ohje/)). A search snippet reports a €9 late-order fee on VALO Tilauspalvelu, but the page could not be confirmed ([valotilaus.fi](https://valotilaus.fi/fi/all/toimitusehdot/)).

Competitors set the benchmark. **Kespro** holds an estimated **49.2%** of the Finnish food-service wholesale peer group ([Kesko FS 2025](https://www.kesko.fi/49309c/globalassets/03-sijoittaja/hallinnointi/2026/2025-kesko-report-by-board-of-directors-and-financial-statements-pdf-eng-id-56346.pdf)). Its Ravintolatukku in the capital region accepts orders until **23:00 for next-day delivery**, delivers twice a day, and gives customers a dedicated hotline. Outside the capital region the cut-off is 15:00–16:00 ([Kespro Ravintolatukku](https://www.kespro.com/palvelut/ravintolatukku)). **Meira Nova**, S Group's captive HoReCa wholesaler, defaults to a 48-hour rhythm, with 24 hours on request and exact time windows as paid extras ([Meira Nova](https://meiranova.fi/palvelut/toimituspalvelut)). Abroad the pattern is the same. Brakes [UK] has a 17:00 cut-off for next-day delivery and emails out-of-stocks the day before so customers can choose alternatives ([Brakes](https://www.brake.co.uk/why-brakes)). Martin & Servera [SE] pushes order-change notices through its app ([Martin & Servera](https://www.martinservera.se/kontakt-och-hjalp/leveransinformation)). Rush capacity is added alongside the main network rather than built into it. Examples are US Foods' Pronto rapid delivery, over $1bn in 2025 sales ([Distribution Strategy Group](https://distributionstrategy.com/2026/02/us-foods-expands-ai-ordering-and-reshapes-sales-model-in-digital-push/)), and Instacart's white-label same-day "will call" delivery for distributors ([Instacart](https://company.instacart.com/instacart-business/buyers-edge-platform-fresh-division-and-instacart-business-partner-to-strengthen-supply-chain-support-for-foodservice-operators-and-distributors)). No Finnish wholesaler publishes an event-order, quote or stock-reservation process.

Ordering technology digitises how a known order arrives, not what should be ordered. **Choco OrderAgent** captures orders from voicemail, WhatsApp, email and photos into distributor ERPs, claiming up to 97% capture accuracy ([Choco](https://choco.com/us/orderagent)). Its **Voice Agent**, launched with OpenAI in December 2025, confirms stock and proposes alternatives live. That is the nearest existing product to rush handling, but it says nothing about allergen-safe substitution ([Choco/OpenAI](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/choco-openai-join-forces-launch-125400570.html)). US Foods' MOXē turns photos and PDFs into orders ([Distribution Strategy Group](https://distributionstrategy.com/2026/02/us-foods-expands-ai-ordering-and-reshapes-sales-model-in-digital-push/)). Sysco's "Swap & Save" substitutions are driven by margin, not exactness ([Distribution Strategy Group](https://distributionstrategy.com/2026/01/sysco-uses-ai-digital-sales-tools-to-boost-case-volumes-as-restaurant-traffic-slows/)). Agentic-commerce protocols handle payment authorisation. Google's UCP had B2B requisitions, approvals and POs only as a charter under review in October 2026 ([scandiweb](https://scandiweb.com/blog/universal-commerce-protocol/)). In Finland, Peppol order and order-response messages are the existing structured rail. Central government has used Peppol Advanced Ordering since April 2024, but only about 32% of wholesale organisations could receive electronic orders ([OpusCapita](https://opuscapita.com/?p=4914)), and there is no public evidence of Peppol ordering in Finnish food service ([../GROUND_TRUTH.md](../GROUND_TRUTH.md)). Nobody publicly offers the full chain. That chain would turn a free-text need such as "lunch for 400, 30 vegan, one nut allergy" into SKUs and quantities, check live stock and lead-time class, commit to a delivery slot before cut-off, and get consent for substitutions before dispatch.

The top segments share four traits that suit the "order or one question" design. First, the need is structured. A menu × covers × diet list maps cleanly to quantities. The exceptions are well bounded: pack-size rounding, stock on hand, and diet sub-counts. Second, the deadline is a known cut-off. Valio Aimo's evening cut-off against Kespro's 23:00 means the correction order is worth most in the few hours after the final count lands. Third, the buyer has no procurement staff and would rather answer one question than build an order. Fourth, the cost of error dwarfs any price difference. An emergency fee of tens of euros is trivial against the roughly €7,000 a 120-cover banquet at €59 brings in. That last figure is illustrative arithmetic from Paasitorni's price list, not a sourced benchmark.

The evidence also shows what the single question should usually be about. It is rarely price. It is a substitution that changes an allergen or organiser constraint, for example "Brand X gluten-free bread is short by 40; the only in-stock alternative contains sesame. Accept, or drop to 20 and top up via Tilaa & Nouda?". It can be a diet sub-count that does not reconcile with the total. Or it can be a delivery promise that misses the event because the item is order-only or the cut-off has passed. Three design implications follow from the failure modes. The agent should treat every substitution on an event order as needing an allergen and spec diff. It should model the base order and the correction order as separate events, diffing one against the other. And it should treat delivery slot, gate and contact person at temporary sites as part of order accuracy. That last point matters at sites with restricted transport windows such as Nokia Arena.

Several uncertainties limit these conclusions. No source confirms which wholesaler supplies Scandic Finland, Lapland Hotels, Restel Food&Events, festival vendors or Leijona's exercise supply. No Nordic data quantifies how often headcounts change after the final guarantee. Willingness to pay for speed is inferred from behaviour (buffers, surcharges, vendor-paid at-sea runs, the €900m direct awards, a UK survey ranking reliability first ([The Caterer/CGA](https://thecaterer.com/all-content/nine-ten-operators-covid-supply-disruption-cga-prestige-survey))), not measured. Valio Aimo's exact cut-off hour, whether delivery is possible on non-agreed days, and any non-public large-order process should be confirmed with Valio Aimo's mentors.

## Conclusion

The research changes the target. The obvious candidates are dramatic emergencies and hard-deadline transport, but the most attractive customer is the ordinary event caterer or banquet kitchen at the moment the final count arrives. That moment recurs weekly across hundreds of Finnish venues, peaks predictably in December, and sits in the gap between a fixed menu and a wholesale cut-off. Today a chef fills that gap by hand, followed by a cash & carry run. Emergencies and strikes are worth supporting as a strategic extension, since wholesalers already sit in HVK's preparedness pool and surge buying flows through existing municipal frameworks. They will not supply a steady stream of orders.

For Valio Aimo, the competitive point is concrete. Kespro owns the visible "event wholesaler" story in Finland and the fastest capital-region cut-off. Valio Aimo's own rush channel drops out-of-stock lines silently. An agent that turns a revised headcount and diet list into a deliverable, allergen-checked order before the evening cut-off would differentiate on the dimension these buyers rank first, and so would a single clear question when no safe alternative exists. A second payoff is recapturing top-up revenue that now leaks to cash & carry. Price optimisation is a secondary feature for these customers.
