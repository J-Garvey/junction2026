## Event ordering process

- Owen reviews the most-ordered drinks beforehand and checks for special event requests.
- He chats directly with event organisers to understand their requirements.
- He orders through a contracted wholesaler: a paid partner arrangement, not a free choice.
- If the order is small and the wholesaler doesn't have something in stock, they go through an off-licence instead, "under the table".

## Wholesale ordering workflow

- He browses the wholesaler's catalogue by hand to place orders. No AI tools were mentioned.
- Key concerns when ordering: allergies, quantities and forecasting.
- He uses a standard forecast for typical event sizes, adjusted after conversations with the client.
- If the wholesaler lacks an item, the contracted partner handles the substitution.

## Order approval and responsibility

- Owen compiles the full order list from the client conversations.
- The restaurant manager reviews and approves every order before it is submitted.
- Sign-off is shared: Owen builds the order, the manager approves it.

## What this suggests for us (team reading)

Not Owen's words, and based on one interview with gaps in the audio:

- **The one-builder, one-approver split matches our approval design.** Someone compiles the order and a manager signs it off, as in [docs/human-approval-rules.md](../docs/human-approval-rules.md) and the purchasing manager and F&B director roles in [ICP.md](../ICP.md).
- **The starting point is a standard forecast per event size**, adjusted by talking to the client. That is the "menu × guests, then adjust" step an agent could take over.
- **A small, out-of-stock gap gets filled outside the contracted wholesaler.** That is the same need as the supermarket top-up in the product scope ([ICP.md](../ICP.md#6-our-product-opportunity)). It also shows the commercial risk: that spend leaves the wholesaler.
- **Substitutions are handled by the wholesaler partner today**, not chosen by the buyer, which fits Valio Aimo's point that substitution is a real operational problem ([research/06](06-valio-aimo-conversation-2026-11-14.md)).
- **Allergies come up unprompted** as one of the three main concerns, alongside quantities and forecasting.

Still unknown from this interview: order sizes and lead times, how often numbers change late, how long building an order takes, and what the manager checks before approving.
