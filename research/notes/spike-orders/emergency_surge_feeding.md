# Emergency and Surge Feeding: How Sudden, Large-Scale Food Needs Are Met (Finland/Nordics priority)

Research date: 14 Nov 2026. Geography is labelled [FI], [SE], [NO], [EU], [UK/US]. Older data is marked with its year. Some facts come from search-result summaries of PDFs that could not be fully fetched. These are marked "(search summary)" and should be re-checked before anyone quotes them.

## 1. Finland: who is responsible, and what structures exist (HVK, municipalities, SPR, Leijona Catering)

### Takeaway
In Finland, emergency food service sits in a layered system. Huoltovarmuuskeskus (HVK) runs a food-supply sector, and its **Ruokapalvelupooli** (food service pool) explicitly includes food-service wholesalers. Municipalities and wellbeing services counties carry the statutory duty to feed people (schools, care, evacuees). SPR/Vapepa volunteers run evacuation-centre feeding on behalf of social authorities. Leijona Catering is the state-owned defence feeding company, and it demands "absolute" delivery reliability from its suppliers. The wholesaler is a named part of this preparedness chain, not an outsider to it.

### Cited Findings
- [FI] HVK's food-supply sector has four pools (primary production, food industry, retail & distribution, food service) plus the KOVA committee for household preparedness — [HVK: Sektori ja poolit](https://www.huoltovarmuuskeskus.fi/toimialat/elintarvikehuolto/elintarvikehuoltosektori)
- [FI] The **Ruokapalvelupooli** exists to secure the continuity of food services in serious disruptions. It supports companies that provide statutory food services to critical actors. Its contracting parties are MaRa ry, Hyvinvointiala HALI ry and Kuntaliitto. Its members include food-service operators, the food industry, **FS wholesalers (FS-tukkukauppa)**, a joint procurement unit, ICT providers, staffing services, filling stations and tourism services. Its tasks are developing preparedness and continuity management, cooperation, awareness, and compiling a sector situational picture — [HVK: Sektori ja poolit](https://www.huoltovarmuuskeskus.fi/toimialat/elintarvikehuolto/elintarvikehuoltosektori)
- [FI] The Kauppa- ja jakelupooli (retail & distribution pool) explicitly covers restaurant and large-kitchen (Food Service) operators. HVK calls retail and distribution the most disruption-sensitive part of the food chain — [HVK: Sektori ja poolit](https://www.huoltovarmuuskeskus.fi/toimialat/elintarvikehuolto/elintarvikehuoltosektori)
- [FI] MaRa announced that food services were added to the security-of-supply organisation by an HVK board decision on 10 January. The year was not visible in the source — [MaRa tiedote](https://www.mara.fi/ajankohtaista/tiedotteet/ruokapalvelut-mukaan-huoltovarmuusorganisaatioon.html) (search summary)
- [FI] HVK has a **Joukkoruokailun valmiussuunnitteluohje (2008)**, a preparedness-planning guide for mass catering — [HVK PDF 2008](https://www.huoltovarmuuskeskus.fi/files/a07eac08f10ec0a6489c4349f3380b0643cb4d37/jrvs_ohje_2008.pdf). There is also a municipal guide, "Ruokapalvelut osana päivittäistavarahuoltoa – varautumisopas kunnille" — [Pelastusopisto PDF](https://www.pelastusopisto.fi/wp-content/uploads/Ruokapalvelut_opas.pdf). Neither was fetchable (certificate error), so their contents are not verified here.
- [FI] HVK's **TIETO26** preparedness exercise (press release 25 Sept 2026) tests whether companies and authorities can keep food supply running in an escalating conflict — [STT Info, 25.9.2026](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/72356720/tieto26-valmiusharjoitus-testaa-yritysten-ja-viranomaisten-kykya-varmistaa-elintarvikehuolto-eskaloituvassa-konfliktissa?lang=fi) (search summary)
- [FI] Valio has published its own article on preparedness planning as the basis of security of supply — [Valio VPP: Varautumissuunnittelu luo huoltovarmuudelle perustan](https://vpp.valio.fi/mjm/artikkelit/varautumissuunnittelu-luo-huoltovarmuudelle-perustan/) (not fetched)
- [FI] SPR's "ensihuolto" (emergency social support) covers emergency accommodation, **muonitus (feeding)**, hygiene, clothing, psychosocial support, special-needs care, phone charging and situational reporting. It runs **in support of social authorities**. The SPR Kalkku logistics centre in Tampere holds the material readiness for evacuation and accommodation — [SPR presentation, M. Asikainen, 4.4.2024](https://rotary.fi/kuusankoski/wp-content/uploads/sites/40/2024/04/SPR-esittely-Mika-Asikainen-04.04.24.pdf) (search summary)
- [FI] The Ministry of Social Affairs and Health tasks SPR with maintaining first-aid, emergency-support and psychosocial-support groups to assist the authorities — [SPR & Vapepa osana kokonaisturvallisuutta, 6.2.2025](https://blogi.eoppimispalvelut.fi/turvallisuusverkosto/files/2025/02/SPR-ja-Vapepa-osana-kokonaisturvallisuutta_turvallisuusverkosto-060225.pdf) (search summary)
- [FI] SPR's 2025 evacuation-centre training ("Avun ketju 2025") sets out the chain of responsibility. Rescue, border and social authorities plan and run evacuations. Evacuation centres are set up jointly with the **wellbeing services county and municipality**. Social and crisis emergency services staff lead the centre, with an SPR/Vapepa volunteer leader under them. "Ruoka" (food) is one of five core functions, alongside registration, psychosocial support, needs assessment and information. A "huolto" (logistics) function explicitly includes food supply (ruokahuolto) — [SPR: Evakuointikeskuksen toiminnot ja johtaminen, AvunKetju2025, 6.10.2025](https://piirit.punainenristi.fi/globalassets/paikallistoiminta/avun-ketju-2025/evakuointikeskuksen-johtaminen-ja-toiminnot_avunketju2025_valmiusharjoitus-06102025.pdf)
- [FI] SPR's Avun ketju 2025 national exercise series included relief-supply distribution, cash aid and food supply (muonitus) as themes — [SPR Avun ketju materials](https://piirit.punainenristi.fi/globalassets/paikallistoiminta/avun-ketju-2025/muut-aineistot/syotekortteja.pdf) (search summary); [Verkkouutiset: SPR rehearses evacuating people for a major power outage](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/spr-harjoittelee-ihmisten-evakuointia-suuren-sahkokatkon-varalta/)
- [FI] **Leijona Catering** is a state-owned company, incorporated in 2012 from the Defence Forces' food service. Its 2022 tender notice says its task is to "secure food supply and continuity of operations in all circumstances" for the Defence Forces and the Criminal Sanctions Agency. It therefore requires from suppliers "absolute reliability, delivery certainty and continuity planning, and testing of the continuity plan" — [TED notice 148698-2022](https://ted.europa.eu/en/notice/148698-2022/pdf) (search summary)
- [FI] A 2025 Defence Forces presentation calls Leijona the PV's strategic partner for garrison feeding and field feeding — [Ruokavirasto/VRN seminar: Sotilaan ravitsemus, 2.12.2025](https://www.ruokavirasto.fi/globalassets/teemat/terveytta-edistava-ruokavalio/ravitsemussitoumus/vrnseminaari_sotilaanravitsemus_02dec25_jakoon.pdf) (search summary). A new Leijona tender notice was published in 2026 — [TED 259929-2026](https://ted.europa.eu/en/notice/259929-2026/pdf) (not fetched)
- [FI] A 2021 survey of tasks for volunteers in exceptional circumstances said Leijona may need volunteers if the Defence Forces raises readiness and mobilises troops — [Reserviläisliitto 2021](https://www.reservilaisliitto.fi/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Alustava_kartoitus_poikkeusolojen_tehtava_vapaaehtoisille_2021.pdf) (search summary)
- [FI] Leijona's tendering has been politically contested on domestic origin. Its dairy tender (Arla vs. Valio) drew criticism from MP Anne Kalmari, and Defence Minister Niinistö replied that Leijona is an independent company (older, c. 2016–2018) — [Verkkouutiset](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/jussi-niinisto-keskustan-anne-kalmari-ampuu-vaaraan-maaliin/)

### Inferences
- FS wholesalers are formally inside HVK's Ruokapalvelupooli. Valio Aimo is therefore plausibly already a preparedness actor, and a surge-order agent could be framed as a continuity tool that fits the pool's tasks (situational picture, continuity management).
- Leijona Catering's "absolute reliability" language and continuity-plan testing are evidence that in defence/preparedness feeding, delivery certainty is a contract criterion alongside price. In peacetime, though, Leijona's daily ordering is regular rather than spiky. The spike comes from readiness raising or mobilisation.
- In an evacuation, the actual "buyer" of food is the wellbeing services county or the municipality, through social emergency services. SPR volunteers serve the food but do not generally hold supplier contracts. An urgent order would most likely come from a municipal or county central kitchen (e.g. Espoo Catering, Kaarea, Servica) that is told to produce extra meals for evacuees.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the contents of the 2008 HVK mass-catering guide or the Pelastusopisto municipal guide (stock days, emergency menus, wholesaler agreements). The fetch failed on a certificate error.
- I found no public figures on how many meals SPR produced in a recent Finnish evacuation, or on whether SPR or municipalities hold standing call-off agreements with wholesalers for evacuation feeding.
- I found no current details of Leijona's crisis-time supply contracts (whether suppliers must hold stock or guarantee lead times).

## 2. Finland: asylum/refugee reception surges (2015 and 2022) — speed, scale, feeding, procurement

### Takeaway
Both surges were large and extremely fast. In 2015, SPR set up 2–4 new reception units **per day** for 25 days, sometimes with **about four hours'** notice, and reached 109 centres and almost 20,000 beds. In 2022, Finland went from 20 centres to 77 within about five months, and Migri then bought nearly €900m of reception services by direct award on urgency grounds. In the first days, feeding was improvised (volunteers, snacks on buses). It then moved either to catered meals or to self-catering on a cash allowance. Speed clearly overrode competitive tendering.

### Cited Findings
- [FI, 2015] Finland received 32,476 asylum seekers in 2015, about ten times a normal year. In late September 2015, up to 600 a day arrived via Tornio, and close to 4,000 were registered in one week — [Finnish Red Cross: An exceptional reception operation (2017, upd. 2021)](https://www.redcross.fi/our-work/domestic/help-for-immigrants/an-exceptional-reception-operation/)
- [FI, 2015] During the Tornio period, SPR set up **2–4 new reception units per day for 25 days**. Emergency accommodation was set up "at a few hours' notice", and volunteers sometimes had **about four hours**. At the peak in early 2016, SPR ran **109 reception centres with almost 20,000 beds**, and it recruited 1,500 new staff in a few months — [Finnish Red Cross](https://www.redcross.fi/our-work/domestic/help-for-immigrants/an-exceptional-reception-operation/)
- [FI, 2015] At the Tornio registration centre, SPR handled accommodation, **food services** and acute health needs. It provided snacks for the long bus transfers, and volunteers prepared meals for arrivals in emergency accommodation. The **Kalkku logistics centre centralised procurement** and designed standard product packages for new units — [Finnish Red Cross](https://www.redcross.fi/our-work/domestic/help-for-immigrants/an-exceptional-reception-operation/)
- [FI, 2015] Asylum seekers moved on average three times in two years, and up to nine times — [Finnish Red Cross](https://www.redcross.fi/our-work/domestic/help-for-immigrants/an-exceptional-reception-operation/)
- [FI, 2015] Private operators entered too. Luona Oy opened Espoo's centre on 16.9.2015 at short notice, the first privately run reception centre in Finland, and a Vantaa centre on 22.9.2015 — [Yle 3-8309618](https://yle.fi/a/3-8309618); [Yle 3-8324100](https://yle.fi/a/3-8324100) (search summary)
- [FI, 2022] By 4 Aug 2022: 35,074 temporary-protection applications, and more than 37,000 clients in the reception system, "more than ever before". There were **77 reception centres** (plus sub-units, private-housing service points and 8 minors' units) **versus 20 centres and 7 minors' units before the invasion**. About 65% lived outside centres, about one-third were children, and monthly applications averaged about 4,500 — [Migri press release 8.8.2022](https://migri.fi/-/yli-35-000-hakenut-tilapaista-suojelua-suomesta-vastaanottojarjestelman-piirissa-enemman-asiakkaita-kuin-koskaan)
- [FI, 2022] Individual centres opened within weeks of the 24 Feb invasion. For example, Espoo started on 16.3.2022 and Pitäjänmäki (500 places) by 22.3.2022 — [Migri: uudet vastaanottokeskukset Espooseen ja Seinäjoelle](https://migri.fi/-/uudet-vastaanottokeskukset-espooseen-ja-seinajoelle-uusia-palvelupisteita-myos-yksityismajoituksessa-oleville); [Migri: perustaa uusia vastaanottokeskuksia](https://migri.fi/-/maahanmuuttovirasto-perustaa-uusia-vastaanottokeskuksia) (search summary)
- [FI, 2022] A Taipalsaari municipal document from spring 2022 put national capacity at about 10,500 places (about 6,300 in use), with more than 13,000 people in private accommodation — [Taipalsaari meeting doc 2022](https://dynastyjulkaisu.taipalsaari.fi/kokous/202271-3-2589.PDF) (search summary)
- [FI] **Feeding model:** some centres provide meals as a reception service, but in many, residents cook for themselves. The basic part of the reception allowance is meant for food when meals are not provided. Indicative monthly amounts: single adult about €300 without meals vs €88 with meals; single parent about €336 vs €97 — [Migri: vastaanottoraha](https://migri.fi/vastaanottoraha); [Migri: arki vastaanottokeskuksessa](https://migri.fi/arki-vastaanottokeskuksessa) (search summary; table garbled, verify)
- [FI, 2022–24] **Price vs speed:** Migri made direct-award (no tender) decisions for reception services worth **almost €900m**. In the first two years of the Ukraine war, direct awards totalled almost €670m (realised costs just over €550m), versus tendered procurements of only just over €70m over two years. More than €200m in direct awards were authorised for 2024. Migri cited the unpredictability of the war-driven crisis and the sudden need to scale capacity in 2022 — [Verkkouutiset reporting Yle MOT, 24.6.2024](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/yle-vastaanottokeskuksiin-kaytettiin-lahes-900-miljoonaa-euroa-ilman-kilpailutusta/)
- [FI] Migri later ran competitive tenders, opening 17 new centres through one round, and corrected some award decisions — [Migri: kilpailutti jälleen – avaa 17 uutta keskusta](https://migri.fi/-/maahanmuuttovirasto-kilpailutti-jalleen-vastaanottokeskustoimintaa-avaa-17-uutta-keskusta); [Migri: oikaisi hankintapäätöksiä](https://migri.fi/-/maahanmuuttovirasto-oikaisi-vastaanottokeskusten-hankintapaatoksia)

### Inferences
- Reception surges create a burst of **new delivery points**: new centres open every day, often in buildings with no kitchen relationship and no account history. They need first-delivery orders with unknown headcounts (hundreds per site) and high shares of children. That is exactly the "large, different every time, needed fast" profile. After the first weeks, demand settles (catering contracts or cash-based self-catering).
- The €900m direct-award episode is strong Finnish evidence that, in a surge, buyers put speed and continuity ahead of competitive price. It also shows that this window is legally time-limited (see section 4).
- Who orders food in a reception surge: the operator of the centre (SPR, municipalities, private firms like Luona), not Migri directly. Migri contracts the operator.

### Gaps
- I found no source describing how 2015 or 2022 centres sourced food in their first 72 hours: from which wholesaler, with what lead time, or how headcount uncertainty was handled. Yle/HS archives for autumn 2015 might hold this but were not reached.
- I found no data on special diets (halal, allergies, infant food) in Finnish reception feeding, and no reported allergen incident.

## 3. Hospital and care surges; other sudden public disruptions (strikes, fires)

### Takeaway
Finnish evidence on hospital kitchens in COVID surges is thin. HUS built a ~150-bed reserve hospital in 2020 that was never used. The best-documented one-off disruptions are **strikes**: in 2024–2025, political and food-industry strikes forced school and daycare kitchens into cold or emergency meals, menu changes and "product shortages and delivery delays" from suppliers. Fires destroy kitchens occasionally, but I found no documented case of outsourced replacement meals.

### Cited Findings
- [FI, 2020] HUS prepared a reserve hospital (varmuussairaala) in a Meilahti parking hall, with 30 tents and about 150 beds. It was not needed in 2020. The source does not mention feeding — [HUS annual report 2020: facility arrangements](https://www.hus.fi/tietoa-meista/vuosikertomukset/2019-2021/2020/en/patient-care/year-of-the-coronavirus-2020/facility-arrangements/index.html) (search summary)
- [UK/US] International hospital foodservice reporting describes COVID surges forcing menu changes, staff retraining and extended hours — [Healthcare Facilities Today: Ramping up foodservice for a pandemic](https://www.healthcarefacilitiestoday.com/posts/Ramping-Up-Foodservice-Operations-for-a-Pandemic--26865); [COVID-19 forces hospital to revamp food service](https://www.healthcarefacilitiestoday.com/posts/COVID-19-Forces-Hospital-To-Revamp-Food-Service--25444) (search summary)
- [FI, 2025] The SEL (Finnish Food Workers' Union) strike of 8–10 April 2025 did not involve Espoo Catering's own staff. It still threatened menus for weeks 15–16 through "product shortages and delivery delays from food producers", and menu changes had to be announced site by site — [Espoo: impacts of spring 2025 strikes on Espoo Catering](https://www.espoo.fi/en/impacts-spring-2025-strikes-on-espoo-caterings-operations)
- [FI, 2024] Espoo published meal-service effects for the political strikes of 13–14 Feb 2024 — [Espoo: effects of political strikes on meal services](https://www.espoo.fi/en/articles/effects-political-strikes-on-meal-services-on-february-13th-14th-2024) (not fetched). University campus restaurants also cut to limited selections because of strikes — [University of Oulu](https://www.oulu.fi/en/news/campus-restaurants-offer-limited-selection-due-strikes)
- [FI] During kitchen strikes, a principal in Kajaani (Laanila school) assembled lunch from the school kitchen's stores, served on disposable dishes, and some pupils brought their own packed lunch — [Kaleva](https://www.kaleva.fi/lakkopaivana-laanilan-koulussa-syotiin-mita-keitti/2930166); [Kaleva: rehtori penkoi evästä](https://www.kaleva.fi/rehtori-penkoi-oppilaille-evasta-lakkopaivana-koul/2931072) (search summary; dates not confirmed)
- [FI] Hollola's Kangas school was destroyed by fire in August 2025. The kitchen and dining wing survived, so meals continued on site. This is not a case of outsourced meals — [Yle 74-20219668](https://yle.fi/a/74-20219668) (search summary)
- [FI] **Allergen/diet incident (school kitchen):** two pupils in Kajaani were hospitalised after an error in food preparation. The date and details were not confirmed — [Kaleva: Kaksi oppilasta sairaalahoitoon Kajaanissa](https://www.kaleva.fi/kaksi-oppilasta-joutui-sairaalahoitoon-kajaanissa/1781304) (search summary)

### Inferences
- Strikes produce predictable spikes: notice is usually days, not hours. They involve ordering substitute products (ready meals, cold items, packed-lunch components) for many sites at once, with special diets still required. A purchasing agent that can re-plan a week's menu around unavailable SKUs, while keeping each site's diet list intact, matches this need.
- A kitchen fire or breakdown at a central production kitchen (which may serve dozens of schools/daycares) would cause an urgent shift to either another kitchen's capacity or purchased ready meals. No Finnish case was documented in my searches.

### Gaps
- I found no Finnish data on wellbeing-services-county hospital kitchens' COVID-era surges (extra meals, quarantine meal delivery, special-diet handling).
- I found no sourced Finnish example of a kitchen fire or equipment failure that forced outsourced meals.

## 4. How surge orders are placed: procurement law, urgency exemptions, frameworks, authority to order

### Takeaway
Finnish and EU procurement law both allow **direct award for "extreme urgency"** caused by unforeseeable events outside the buyer's control. The exemption is interpreted narrowly and must last only as long as strictly necessary. The Commission's 2020 COVID guidance calls it a stop-gap until a framework is in place. In practice, surge buying therefore happens either (a) as call-offs under existing wholesaler framework agreements, or (b) as short direct awards that are later tendered. Finnish regulators (KKV, Market Court) do penalise over-long urgency awards.

### Cited Findings
- [FI] Hankintalaki (1397/2016) §40(2)(4) allows a negotiated procedure without prior notice (suorahankinta) where the contract is strictly necessary and the statutory time limits cannot be met **due to extreme urgency caused by unforeseeable events not attributable to the contracting authority** — summarised in [KKV decision r-2025-60-0106](https://www.kkv.fi/uploads/sites/2/r-2025-60-0106.pdf) and theses at [Univ. of Vaasa](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/eaf8cfd8-afdb-494a-a16c-9f62154395db/content) (search summary; check wording on Finlex)
- [FI] The Market Court has accepted extreme urgency as a ground but found that a direct award's **duration exceeded what was strictly necessary** — [kohenavocats summary of KHO 8.10.2025/2250](https://kohenavocats.com/jurisprudences/kho-8-10-2025-2250-julkista-hankintaa-koskeva-valituslupahakemus-ja-valitus-henkilostohallinnon-tukipalvelut-sidosyksikkoaseman-arvioiminen/) (search summary)
- [FI] KKV (Finnish Competition and Consumer Authority) has issued a remark over school-transport direct awards justified by §40 extreme urgency (Sastamala). It has also proposed a penalty fee for a joint municipal authority (Kymenlaakso) that made a direct award without legal ground — [KKV supervision reporting 2023](https://www.kkv.fi/uploads/sites/2/julkisten-hankintojen-valvonnan-tulosten-raportointi-2023.pdf); [KKV r-2022-60-0020](https://www.kkv.fi/uploads/sites/2/r-2022-60-0020.pdf) (search summary)
- [FI] Contracts for social and health services, which reception services resemble, must be tendered above €400,000. They may be awarded without tendering only if the authority lacks time for reasons not attributable to itself — [Verkkouutiset/Yle MOT 2024](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/yle-vastaanottokeskuksiin-kaytettiin-lahes-900-miljoonaa-euroa-ilman-kilpailutusta/)
- [EU, 2020] The Commission Communication 2020/C 108 I/01 (1 Apr 2020) sets out three options: accelerated open/restricted procedures with shortened deadlines, and the **negotiated procedure without publication**. The negotiated route allows buying "without time limits, minimum number of candidates or other procedural requirements" where events are unforeseeable and extreme urgency makes the general deadlines impossible. It should cover only **stop-gap needs until more stable solutions such as framework contracts** — [Bevan Brittan summary](https://www.bevanbrittan.com/insights/articles/2020/procurement-european-commission-communication-on-public-procurement-in-the-covid-19-emergency-situation-2020c-108-101/); [A&L Goodbody](https://www.algoodbody.com/insights-publications/covid-19-european-commission-publishes-guidance-on-public-procurement); [EC guidance text (minfin.bg copy)](https://aeuf.minfin.bg/upload/15112/Guidance+from+the+European+Commission+on+using+the+public+procurement+framework+in+the.pdf)
- [EU] Commentators report that accelerated procedures can cut tender deadlines to about 15 days (open) and 10 days (restricted). These figures were not verified against the primary text — [A&L Goodbody](https://www.algoodbody.com/insights-publications/covid-19-european-commission-publishes-guidance-on-public-procurement) (search summary)
- [EU] National and EU courts consistently say the negotiated-without-publication ground must be interpreted narrowly — [McCann FitzGerald](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/covid-19/covid-19-european-commission-publishes-guidance-on-the-use-of-the-public-procurement-framework-in-the-crisis) (search summary)

### Inferences
- Even a 10–15-day accelerated tender is far too slow for "meals tonight". So in the first hours and days of a surge, public buyers rely on **existing framework agreements with wholesalers** (municipal/county food-supply frameworks, often via joint procurement units such as Hansel or regional units). The surge then shows up as unusually large and unusual call-offs on an already-contracted wholesaler. This strongly favours an incumbent wholesaler with an ordering channel that works under pressure.
- Authority to order in a surge is likely to sit with the operational kitchen manager or the social emergency service leading the evacuation centre, under the municipal/county framework. The legal analysis of who may sign a §40 direct award is not covered by my sources.
- Headcount estimation: no source described a formal method. In 2015 SPR relied on standard product packages from Kalkku, which suggests pre-defined kits per unit rather than bespoke calculation. That kit model is inferred from the Red Cross description and could translate into "order templates per N persons".

### Gaps
- I found no Finnish source on how municipal food frameworks handle emergency call-offs: guaranteed lead times, emergency delivery surcharges, or out-of-hours ordering.
- I found no documented method for quantity estimation under uncertain headcounts in the Finnish/Nordic context. Humanitarian norms (e.g. Sphere 2,100 kcal/person/day) were not verified in this session.

## 5. Nordic and EU comparators (Sweden MSB/Livsmedelsverket, Norway DSB)

### Takeaway
Sweden is formalising municipal crisis-meal planning. About one-third of municipalities still lacked one or more crisis food/meal plans in 2024 (down from 60% in 2021), and MSB was named national coordinator of supply preparedness in June 2025. Norway places evacuee feeding (forpleining) on the municipality, in support of the police. Neither country's public material I found names wholesaler agreements for surge feeding.

### Cited Findings
- [SE, 2024] Livsmedelsverket's "Fakta om offentliga måltider 2024" reports that about a third of municipalities still lack one or more plans for food supply and meals in a crisis, down from 60% in 2021 — [Livsmedelsverket L 2025 nr 14](https://www.livsmedelsverket.se/globalassets/publikationsdatabas/rapporter/2025/l-2025-nr-14---fakta-om-offentliga-maltider-2024.pdf) (search summary; PDF text not extractable)
- [SE] Media framing: 1.5 million Swedes in preschool, school and elder care depend on public meals, yet a third of municipalities lack a crisis plan for food — [Dagens PS: Var tredje kommun saknar krisplan för mat](https://www.dagensps.se/varlden/var-tredje-kommun-saknar-krisplan-for-mat/) (search summary)
- [SE, 2025] The Swedish government appointed MSB as national coordinator of Sweden's supply preparedness (June 2025) — [Regeringen press release 2025-06](https://regeringen.se/pressmeddelanden/2025/06/regeringen-utser-msb-till-nationell-samordnare-av-sveriges-forsorjningsberedskap/) (search summary)
- [SE] The defence bill set a planning goal that food supply should withstand at least three months of a security-policy crisis with serious disruption — [Livsmedelsverket 2021: En robust livsmedelsförsörjning vid kriser och höjd beredskap](https://www.livsmedelsverket.se/globalassets/publikationsdatabas/rapporter/2021/2020_03221-en-robust-livsmedelsforsorjning-vid-kriser-och-hojd-beredskap.pdf) (search summary)

## 6. Diets, allergens, lead times, substitution and the role of price

### Takeaway
Direct evidence is sparse. The strongest sourced signals are these. Surge populations contain many vulnerable people (about one-third children among 2022 Ukrainian arrivals in Finland). Evacuation centres explicitly triage people with special needs at entry. Strikes cause supplier shortages that force site-by-site menu changes. Price is subordinated to speed in emergency procurement (the €900m direct awards, Leijona's reliability requirements). I found no published Finnish data on allergen handling or allergen incidents in surge feeding.

### Cited Findings
- [FI, 2022] About one-third of Ukrainian temporary-protection arrivals were children — [Migri 8.8.2022](https://migri.fi/-/yli-35-000-hakenut-tilapaista-suojelua-suomesta-vastaanottojarjestelman-piirissa-enemman-asiakkaita-kuin-koskaan)
- [FI] In SPR evacuation-centre doctrine, the reception stage aims to identify "those in particular need of help". A needs assessment covers basic needs, first aid and material aid before food is served. The support functions plan for a child-friendly space, an isolation room and pets — [SPR AvunKetju2025, 6.10.2025](https://piirit.punainenristi.fi/globalassets/paikallistoiminta/avun-ketju-2025/evakuointikeskuksen-johtaminen-ja-toiminnot_avunketju2025_valmiusharjoitus-06102025.pdf). SPR's ensihuolto definition explicitly includes "erityistarpeista huolehtiminen" (caring for special needs) — [SPR presentation 2024](https://rotary.fi/kuusankoski/wp-content/uploads/sites/40/2024/04/SPR-esittely-Mika-Asikainen-04.04.24.pdf) (search summary)
- [FI] Lead times achieved: SPR emergency accommodation was stood up in **about four hours** at times in 2015, with 2–4 units per day for 25 days — [Finnish Red Cross](https://www.redcross.fi/our-work/domestic/help-for-immigrants/an-exceptional-reception-operation/). In 2022, new centres opened about 3–4 weeks after the invasion (16–22 March 2022) — [Migri](https://migri.fi/-/uudet-vastaanottokeskukset-espooseen-ja-seinajoelle-uusia-palvelupisteita-myos-yksityismajoituksessa-oleville)

- [EU] Emergency direct award is legally framed as a stop-gap. Speed justifies skipping competition only for the strictly necessary period — [Bevan Brittan on EC 2020/C 108 I/01](https://www.bevanbrittan.com/insights/articles/2020/procurement-european-commission-communication-on-public-procurement-in-the-covid-19-emergency-situation-2020c-108-101/); [KHO 2025/2250 summary](https://kohenavocats.com/jurisprudences/kho-8-10-2025-2250-julkista-hankintaa-koskeva-valituslupahakemus-ja-valitus-henkilostohallinnon-tukipalvelut-sidosyksikkoaseman-arvioiminen/)

### Inferences
- **Failure modes** suggested by the evidence:
  - Headcount uncertainty and repeated relocation (people moved three to nine times) mean order quantities keep changing.
  - New, unknown delivery addresses opened within hours.
  - Out-of-hours timing.
  - Volunteer-run kitchens without allergen-management routines.
  - Supplier shortages during strikes that force substitutions which may break diet or allergen constraints.

  These are inferred from the sources above, not reported as incidents.
- For an AI purchasing agent, the value in surge segments is likely to come from:
  - Turning "N people, X% children, known diets" into a complete, allergen-safe order fast.
  - Proposing allergen-equivalent substitutes when SKUs are short.
  - Handling new delivery points.
  - Leaving an audit trail, which urgency direct awards need because the justification must be documented.

  Price sensitivity is low in the first days and returns quickly once frameworks or tenders follow.
- Volume: Finnish surges are episodic. There were two major reception surges in a decade (2015, 2022), plus strikes in 2024–2025. The steady customer base behind them is the municipal/county central kitchens that absorb surges, not a separate "emergency buyer" segment.

- I did not verify IFRC/Sphere feeding standards or academic humanitarian-logistics literature in this session.
