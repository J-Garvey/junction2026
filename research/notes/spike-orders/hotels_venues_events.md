# Hotels, conference centres and event/banquet venues: sudden, large, one-off food orders under time pressure

Scope note: research done 14 Nov 2026 with about 20 searches and fetches. The search tool mostly returned summarised snippets, so the primary pages behind snippet-only claims were not always opened. Those claims are marked "(snippet)" and should be checked before quoting. Each fact is labelled with its geography: [FI] Finland, [UK], [US], [Global/vendor].

## 1. What triggers spikes, how short the notice is, and how often headcounts change late

### Takeaway
Most hotel/venue food spikes are **booked events with a contract**: banquets, meetings, Christmas parties, weddings, trade fairs and Lapland charter-season groups. What arrives at short notice is the **final number and the diet list**, not the event itself. In Finland, Sokos Hotels asks for menu and special diets **14 days** before arrival and allows partial free reductions until **1–14 days** before, depending on group size. In the US/UK, final guarantees are typically due **48–72 hours** before. Actual turnout differs from registration by **~17–28%** (median no-show, paid vs free events), so the last-minute quantity correction is the real spike.

### Cited Findings
- [FI] Sokos Hotels group terms: the programme, guest names and meal/special-diet details (one menu per group) must be sent **at least 14 days before arrival**. Free change/cancellation depends on group size and the share of the booking. Under 15 people: 100% free until 7 days, 50% until 5, 25% until 3, 10% until 1 day before. 61–120 people: 100%/50%/25%/10% at 42/28/14/7 days. 201–350 people: 84/56/42/14 days. Groups over 350 are agreed case by case. The price follows the confirmed number, or the actual number if higher. No-shows and early leavers are charged in full, and the hotel is entitled to full compensation for products ordered in advance, including subcontracted ones. — [Sokos Hotels group terms](https://www.sokoshotels.fi/en/group-terms)
- [FI] Paasitorni (Helsinki Congress) asks customers to pick one menu per group and to notify special diets "in advance", with no stated deadline. Minimum billing is 20–30 persons, depending on the menu. — [Paasitorni catering for meetings and banquets](https://paasitorni.fi/en/restaurants/paasiravintola/catering-for-meetings-and-banquets)
- [US] Final-guarantee deadlines vary: ASU Catering wants a guaranteed count **3 business days** before (otherwise the estimate becomes final), other policies use **48 hours**, and some group policies use **14 calendar days**. Billing is usually "guarantee or number served, whichever is greater". (snippet) — [ASU Catering policies](https://sundevilhospitality.asu.edu/catering/asu-catering-policies-and-guidelines); [CMU catering policies 2026](https://www.cmu.edu/dining/catering/catering-policies-page-2026.pdf)
- [US] Caterers commonly **prepare 5% over the final guarantee**, capped at 30–50 meals. Late increases carry surcharges: a 10% surcharge on increases over 10% of the guarantee, and a **50% surcharge for same-day increases**. (snippet, venue policies aggregated by search) — [Law Insider guaranteed attendance clauses](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/guaranteed-attendance/_2); [CMU catering policies](https://www.cmu.edu/dining/catering/catering-policies_chartwells-2022.pdf)
- [Global/vendor] Check-in data from Pheedloop's Event Data Lab gives a **median no-show of ~28% for free events and ~17% for paid events**, and **~37%** for free events with 10–49 attendees. It recommends planning catering for 80% (paid) or 70% (free) of registrations. — [Pheedloop Event Data Lab #05](https://pheedloop.com/blog/event-data-lab-report-5)
- [Global/vendor] Conference organisers typically plan catering against a ~15% no-show forecast. Other vendor estimates for free events run 30–60%, with no disclosed method. (snippet) — [Ticket Fairy no-show estimator](https://www.ticketfairy.com/tools/no-show-rate-estimator); [Eventtia](https://www.eventtia.com/how-to-prevent-no-shows-at-your-events-tips-and-strategies/)
- [FI] Lapland Christmas peak: 1 Dec–6 Jan is the core window, with many charter flights from the UK, Sweden and elsewhere. Rovaniemi Airport passed **1 million annual passengers** for the first time in 2025, and 37 routes were announced for winter 2026–27. (snippet, travel trade press) — [Travel And Tour World](https://www.travelandtourworld.com/news/article/u1qwcmzutepi/)
- [FI] Statistics Finland, Dec 2025: **1.93 million** overnight stays nationally, and for the first time foreign stays (0.98 m) exceeded domestic stays (0.95 m). International tourism is concentrated in Lapland and Uusimaa, and foreign stays in Lapland kept growing. Dec 2024: Lapland foreign stays were **0.52 m (+9% y/y)**. — [Statistics Finland, accommodation statistics Dec 2025](https://stat.fi/fi/julkaisu/cmfzaqf1o2m9e07uq8doe39sl); [Statistics Finland Dec 2024](https://stat.fi/fi/julkaisu/cm1hyrjb24zwc07w5giiwybu7)
- [FI] Yle: Lapland passed Uusimaa in December, with over 620,000 stays in Lapland vs ~550,000 in Uusimaa, and a Rovaniemi record of over 166,000 stays. The snippet does not make clear which period these figures cover. — [Yle](https://yle.fi/a/74-20208432)
- [FI] The average Lapland hotel-room price in Dec 2025 was **€356**, vs a Finland average of **€168** (Statistics Finland, as reported). — [Statistics Finland news](https://stat.fi/fi/uutinen/Lapin-hotellit-joulukuussa-ennaetyksellisen-kalliita-taustalla-ulkomaisten-matkailijoiden-lisaeaentyminen); [STT](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70860019/lapin-hotellit-joulukuussa-ennatyksellisen-kalliita-taustalla-ulkomaisten-matkailijoiden-lisaantyminen?lang=fi)
- [FI] Paasitorni's 2026 sheet has a dedicated Christmas season: a Christmas buffet (from €59, minimum 30) and a "Traditional Christmas lunch, 14–18 Dec 2026, €55". This shows the pikkujoulu (December Christmas-party) peak as a menu product. — [Paasitorni catering](https://paasitorni.fi/en/restaurants/paasiravintola/catering-for-meetings-and-banquets)
- [FI] Messukeskus Helsinki (trade fairs) has **21 cafés and restaurants**, and its restaurant/catering partner is NoHo Partners. An undated brochure cites in-house catering for "10,000 delegates". (snippet) — [Messukeskus services](https://messukeskus.com/en/for-event-organizers/event-services/?lang=en); [Messukeskus brochure (SlideShare)](https://www.slideshare.net/Messukeskus/messukeskus-helsinki-brochure-67584750)

### Inferences
- In this segment, an AI purchasing agent's spike is the **two-stage order**: a base order placed about 1–2 weeks out, when the menu and diets are fixed (14 days for Sokos), then a correction order **1–3 days out**, when the final numbers land. The correction order is where speed and accuracy matter most.
- Venue contracts already move the financial risk of no-shows onto the client ("charged in full"), so the venue's main exposure is **under-supply**, not over-supply. Running short when headcount rises late, or when diet counts change, costs reputation, and venues cannot pass that cost on.
- In Lapland, December is a predictable seasonal surge, not a surprise. It sits on top of thin last-mile logistics and Christmas holiday closures (24–26 Dec), so the hard constraint there is delivery windows, not notice.

### Gaps
- I found no Finnish or Nordic data on **how often** event headcounts change after the final guarantee, or by how much. The US/vendor no-show data is a proxy.
- No data on walk-in surges at Helsinki hotels during city events (Slush, concerts at Olympic Stadium, cruise days). None was found in this pass.
- Finlandia Hall and Tampere-talo catering operators and terms were not found.

## 2. Today's order flow: BEOs, roles, par levels, central vs per-property purchasing, systems

### Takeaway
The standard internationally is that sales/catering creates a **Banquet Event Order (BEO)**, also called a function sheet, recording the menu, guarantee, timings and diets. The kitchen (exec chef/sous chef) translates it into production and purchase lists. In most properties this step is still semi-manual, with procurement tools able to automate it from recipes. For Finnish chains (Scandic, Sokos), I found no public documentation of the internal ordering workflow. S Group's hotels have a captive wholesaler in Meira Nova.

### Cited Findings
- [Global] The BEO is the document that records the agreed guarantee, timings, menu and charges, and it is the operational instruction to the kitchen and banquet teams. (snippet) — [BoomPop help: what is a BEO](https://help.boompop.com/en/articles/11635758-what-is-a-banquet-event-order-beo-and-why-does-it-matter)
- [Global/vendor] Apicbase (hotel F&B software): production planning turns bills of materials into **purchase orders for each supplier**, and recipes scale automatically to the requested portions. POs can be generated from PAR levels, a bill of materials, production plans, menu cycles or forecast demand. Its catering module "consolidates customer orders into detailed workplans for batch production". None of the pages mentions BEOs by name. (snippet) — [Apicbase production planning](https://get.apicbase.com/production-planning/); [Apicbase procurement](https://get.apicbase.com/procurement-management/); [Apicbase catering](https://get.apicbase.com/catering-software/); [Apicbase hotels](https://get.apicbase.com/hotels/)
- [US/vendor] Galley says its platform scales recipes, manages event orders, automates production lists, and controls purchasing and inventory. (snippet) — [Galley vs Apicbase](https://www.galleysolutions.com/galley-vs-apicbase)
- [FI] Valio Aimo's delivery-wholesale webshop supports planning orders **several weeks ahead using multiple shopping carts**. Valio Aimo also offers the VALO Tilauspalvelu ordering service, whose users must be set up as wholesale customers. — [Valio Aimo delivery wholesale](https://www.valioaimo.fi/en/become-a-customer/delivery-wholesale/)
- [FI] Meira Nova Oy is SOK's (S Group's) subsidiary for purchasing, customer service and logistics of perishables and consumables in HoReCa. Its customers include hotels and restaurants, staff canteens, the public sector and catering kitchens. It distributes nationwide from a logistics centre in Tuusula plus local terminals. (snippet) — [Optiscan reference story: Meira Nova](https://optiscangroup.com/reference/reference-story-meira-nova); [KKV filing (Meira Nova turnover ~€361m in 2016)](https://www.kkv.fi/uploads/sites/2/3b34f7d99ef345878d8ba4dd7ae86c16.pdf)
- [FI] Sokotel Oy (S Group) operates Sokos Hotels; according to the snippet, 14 Sokos Hotels, seven Radisson Blu hotels and Radisson RED Helsinki in Finland. Its restaurants focus on Finnish ingredients and follow S Group's sustainable-procurement principles. (snippet) — [Sokos Hotels sustainable brand index page](https://www.sokoshotels.fi/en/sustainable-brand-index)
- [FI] Since 1 Jul 2025, Restel's Food&Events Tapahtumaravintolat unit has been the exclusive caterer for all Paasitorni restaurants, replacing Graniittiravintolat Oy. Scandic Paasi hotel is adjacent. — [Paasitorni: new restaurant operator](https://www.paasitorni.fi/en/a-new-restaurant-operator-to-take-over-paasitorni-restaurants/)
- [FI] A Finnish head-chef job description puts supplier relationships and coordination with event/catering teams inside the chef role. This points to chef-led purchasing rather than a separate buyer at property level. (snippet, a single job ad) — [laura.fi job ad](https://laura.fi/avoimet-tyopaikat/cafe-logo-oy/keittiomestari-kokki/3299468/)
- [FI] Scandic Finland switched its coffee supplier to Löfbergs Lila, which shows chain-level supplier agreements for some categories. (older press release, date not verified) — [Mynewsdesk Löfbergs](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/lofbergs/pressreleases/finnish-hotel-giant-switches-to-loefbergs-lila-747361)

### Inferences
- The likely pattern in Finnish chains is **central chain agreements** (supplier, range, price) with **per-property ordering by the chef or kitchen manager** within those agreements. This is consistent with the coffee example and the S Group/Meira Nova captive structure, but it is not confirmed for food ordering at Scandic or Sokos.
- Sokos/Radisson Blu (Sokotel) properties are probably served mainly by Meira Nova, which makes them a harder target for Valio Aimo than Scandic, independents (Kämp Collection, Lapland Hotels) or venue caterers (Restel Food&Events, NoHo at Messukeskus). This is my inference and is not confirmed.

### Gaps
- No public source found on Scandic Finland's or Lapland Hotels' food wholesaler(s) or ordering systems (e.g. Jamix, Apicbase, Oracle Opera Sales & Catering, Delphi). Trade press such as Kehittyvä Ruokapalvelu, Restauranttivisio and MaRa did not surface in searches.
- No documentation found of how Finnish hotels connect a sales & catering system to the kitchen ordering process.

## 3. Translating an event (menu × covers) into an ingredient order, and how manual it is

### Takeaway
The conversion is recipe scaling (bill of materials × covers + buffer) followed by grouping by supplier. Software can automate it, but only if recipes are fully costed and maintained. The available evidence describes it as a software capability, not a measured adoption rate.

### Cited Findings
- [Global/vendor] Apicbase states that recipes scale automatically to portions and that the bill of materials converts into per-supplier POs. (snippet) — [Apicbase production planning](https://get.apicbase.com/production-planning/)
- [US] A buffer is built in on purpose: prepare 5% over guarantee, capped at 30–50 meals. (snippet) — [CMU catering policies](https://www.cmu.edu/dining/catering/catering-policies-page-2026.pdf)
- [FI] Venues standardise to reduce complexity: "one menu per group" (Sokos, Paasitorni) and per-piece minimums for canapés (from €2, minimum 20 pieces per variety at Paasitorni). — [Sokos group terms](https://www.sokoshotels.fi/en/group-terms); [Paasitorni catering](https://paasitorni.fi/en/restaurants/paasiravintola/catering-for-meetings-and-banquets)

### Inferences
- "Menu × covers → order" is well defined enough for an agent to do. The hard parts are the exceptions: diet sub-counts (e.g. 7 gluten-free, 3 vegan, 1 nut allergy), pack-size rounding, what is already in stock, and lead-time differences between products.

### Gaps
- No survey data found on the share of hotel/venue kitchens using recipe-based automated ordering vs spreadsheets/manual, in Finland or elsewhere.

## 4. Supplier side: cut-offs, emergency deliveries, cash & carry, multiple suppliers, substitutions, allergens

### Takeaway
Finnish wholesale delivers next-day. Valio Aimo promises **24-hour** delivery on most of its ~25,000 SKUs, and Kespro's restaurant-wholesale model uses a **15:00 cut-off for next-morning** delivery in regional cities. Same-day needs fall back on cash & carry. I found no published same-day express service from Finnish foodservice wholesalers.

### Cited Findings
- [FI] Valio Aimo delivery wholesale: about 25,000 products, warehouses in Espoo and Kuopio, deliveries on agreed delivery days, and most products available within a 24-hour delivery window. No express/same-day or minimum order stated on the page. Customers include hotels, restaurants, catering and large kitchens. Valio Aimo also runs Cash & Carry stores. — [Valio Aimo delivery wholesale](https://www.valioaimo.fi/en/become-a-customer/delivery-wholesale/); [Valio Aimo in English](https://valioaimo.fi/valio-aimo-in-english)
- [FI] Kespro Ravintolatukku (Jyväskylä, Kuopio, Oulu): order by **15:00 the previous day** for next-morning delivery. The model was launched in the Helsinki region in early 2021 and later extended to Lahti, Turku and Tampere. Kespro also mentions ordering non-standard delivery times by a set deadline (no times given). (snippet, article undated) — [Kespro fast ordering model](https://www.kespro.com/sv/ideer-och-inspiration/artiklar/nopea-tilausmalli-lanseerataan-jyvaskylan-kuopion-ja-oulun); [Kespro delivery services](https://www.kespro.com/ideat-ja-inspiraatiot/artikkelit/toimituspalvelut-kespro-logistiikkapalvelu)
- [FI] Herkkukonttori, a Finnish foodservice wholesaler, says a hotel-buffet volume rush differs from a restaurant rush, and that catering events need different logistics from weekly stock-ups. (snippet) — [Herkkukonttori](https://herkkukonttori.fi/mita-ravintolatukku-tarkoittaa)
- [FI] In Lapland at Christmas, supermarkets close early on Christmas Eve and Alko is closed 24–26 Dec. This limits emergency retail top-ups during peak. (snippet, travel guide) — [Hungry Travel Family Lapland guide](https://hungrytravelfamily.com/blog/lapland/lapland-christmas-season-guide-when-to-go-and-what-to-book/)

### Inferences
- With a D-1 15:00 cut-off and final guarantees arriving 1–3 days out, there is little room for a correction order: it must be placed within hours of the final count. An agent that turns a revised count into a submitted order straight away, before the cut-off, captures that value.
- Substitutions matter for events: a wholesaler substitution on an allergen-relevant item, such as a different bread or sauce brand, can invalidate the allergen information the venue has already given guests. Substitution rules per event or order line, e.g. "no substitution" or "substitute only with an allergen-equivalent item", look like a real feature need. This is my inference, and I found no source documenting such incidents.

### Gaps
- Exact cut-off times for Valio Aimo, Meira Nova and Kespro in the Helsinki region, and whether any of them sells paid same-day/express delivery, are not published on the pages I checked. Other strands, or direct contact, should confirm.
- No data on how often hotels/venues use cash & carry or multiple suppliers for event top-ups.

## 5. Failure modes and their cost

### Takeaway
Allergen failures at events are the best-documented and most costly failure: UK prosecutions over wedding canapés led to fines of about £16k plus costs. The financial risk of over-ordering is largely pushed onto clients by contract. Running out has no quantified cost in public sources.

### Cited Findings
- [UK] Hipping Hall (Cumbria/Lancaster), Dec 2021: the best man at a wedding suffered **anaphylaxis from a canapé containing nuts**. The operator pleaded guilty under food hygiene regulations and was **fined £16,000 + £190 victim surcharge + £7,071.41 costs** (2023). The judge cited a "realistic risk of death". — [Lancaster City Council](https://www.lancaster.gov.uk/news/2023/sep/food-business-fined-after-man-suffers-a-severe-allergic-reaction-at-wedding); [Anaphylaxis UK](https://www.anaphylaxis.org.uk/cumbria-hotel-fined-16000-after-best-man-suffers-anaphylaxis-at-wedding)
- [UK] 2017: a catering company was fined after a bride with a nut allergy was served a wedding cake containing almonds. — [Trading Standards 2017](https://www.tradingstandards.uk/news-policy/news-room/2017/catering-company-fined-after-bride-suffers-allergic-reaction-on-her-wedding-day)
- [UK] Wider context (not event-specific): a restaurant owner was jailed for six years for manslaughter by gross negligence after a peanut-allergy death. A London café operator was fined after a girl's death from an allergic reaction. — [VWA blog](https://www.vwa.co.uk/blog/restaurant-owner-jailed-use-allergen-food/); [The Caterer](https://thecaterer.com/all-content/london-cafe-fined-following-death-of-girl-from-allergic-reaction)
- [FI] Contract terms protect against over-ordering: Sokos charges no-shows in full and claims full compensation for products ordered in advance. — [Sokos group terms](https://www.sokoshotels.fi/en/group-terms)
- [US] Venue contracts price late changes on purpose, with a 50% surcharge on same-day increases. (snippet) — [CMU catering policies](https://www.cmu.edu/dining/catering/catering-policies-page-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The asymmetry is strong. Over-ordering is partly recoverable (billed to the client, or reused in breakfast or lunch buffets). Under-ordering, or an allergen error, is visible, carries legal risk, and damages a high-value relationship (weddings, corporate repeat business). This supports the brief's claim that accuracy matters more than price here.

### Gaps
- No Finnish/Nordic allergen incident data for hotel/event catering was found; Ruokavirasto or municipal food-control reports were not searched.
- No survey quantifying how often events run out of items, or the cost of emergency purchases.
- No banquet-specific food-waste figures found.

## 6. Price sensitivity: are event and banquet purchases less price-sensitive?

### Takeaway
Banquet food is sold per cover at fixed prices set well in advance; in Finland that is about €55–59+ per person for buffets or set menus. Banquets are generally cited as the highest-margin part of hotel F&B. Ingredient cost is a modest share of the price, so a small premium for fast, exact delivery is easy to absorb. Hard, audited banquet-only margin benchmarks were not found.

### Cited Findings
- [FI] Paasitorni 2026 per-person prices (VAT status not specified on the page): buffets from **€59** (minimum charge 30), served set menus from **€59** (minimum 20), cocktail/snack selections from €30, Christmas lunch €55, night snack buffet from €15, coffee + pastry €11.90, all-day meeting-room products €17. — [Paasitorni catering](https://paasitorni.fi/en/restaurants/paasiravintola/catering-for-meetings-and-banquets)
- [US] CBRE Trends: hotel F&B departmental profit margin was **29.3% in 2022**, **28.3% YTD June 2023**, and **~29.1% in H1 2025** (as reported in trade press). — [Hotel Management / CBRE](https://www.hotelmanagement.net/food-beverage/cbre-hotel-food-and-beverage-expense-revenue-shifts-boost-profits); [CBRE brief](https://www.cbre.com/insights/briefs/hotel-financial-performance-influenced-by-changes-in-hotel-food-and-beverage-offerings); [Hotel-Online 2025](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/hotel-food-and-beverage-a-bright-spot-in-2025)
- [Global, secondary] Banquet and catering is described as the most profitable part of hotel F&B because it is pre-planned. One guide puts banquet margins at 30–40%, but the figure is unsourced and should be treated as directional only. (snippet) — [Prostay guide](https://www.prostay.com/blog/food-and-beverage-service-in-hotel/); [apers: full- vs select-service](https://apers.app/learn/asset-classes/hospitality/full-service-vs-select-vs-limited-operating-models)
- [FI] At peak, Lapland demand supports premium pricing: the average December 2025 hotel-room price was €356, vs €168 nationally. — [Statistics Finland](https://stat.fi/fi/uutinen/Lapin-hotellit-joulukuussa-ennaetyksellisen-kalliita-taustalla-ulkomaisten-matkailijoiden-lisaeaentyminen)

### Inferences
- The client price is locked per cover at booking, so ingredient costs cannot be passed on after the fact. Paying an express premium on a correction order is still small next to the per-cover revenue. Illustration (my inference, not sourced): 120 covers × €59 ≈ €7,000 of revenue, against an emergency delivery fee of tens of euros.
- Lapland's December pricing (room rates more than 2× the national average) suggests operators there will pay for reliability at peak.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence found (survey or wholesaler data) on whether hotels/venues pay for express delivery, or on their price elasticity for event orders compared with routine orders.
- No Finnish data on food cost as a percentage of banquet price.
