# Transport and Remote-Site Catering: Large, Variable, Time-Critical Food Orders

Scope note: research done 14 Nov 2026 with a budget of about 17 search/fetch calls. Several sources reached me only as search-result summaries, because a full fetch failed or was not tried. These are marked "(snippet)" and should be re-checked before anyone quotes them. Every fact is labelled with its geography and year.

## 1. Airline catering: load changes up to departure, special meals, flight-kitchen supply

### Takeaway
Flight kitchens plan production on forecasts days ahead, but the final load is not known until hours before departure. Special-meal requests close at a hard cut-off, usually 24 h before departure (Finnair, Delta, Cathay; global). In Helsinki, flight catering sits with Finnair Kitchen (about 400 staff) and LSG Sky Chefs / Star Food Finland (about 8,000 meals a day). These are concentrated, high-volume buyers. No public source shows how they buy raw ingredients from wholesalers or what their order cut-offs are.

### Cited Findings
- **Finnair Kitchen (Vantaa, FI, current site):** about 400 professionals covering product development, goods reception, kitchen, packaging and loading. Dozens of seasonal workers are hired each year, mainly for summer. The page gives no meal counts and names no suppliers. — [Finnair Kitchen in a nutshell](https://company.finnair.com/en/finnair-kitchen-in-a-nutshell-2781938)
- Finnair material says "several thousand meals" leave Finnair Kitchen every day (FI; snippet). — [Finnair company site, Finnair Kitchen](https://company.finnair.com/en/finnair-kitchen-2781930)
- An older, undated blog figure says Finnair Catering had about 510 staff and made about 12,000 meals a day (FI; probably before the ownership changes, so treat as historical). — [inflightfeed.com](https://inflightfeed.com/?p=16632) (snippet)
- LSG Sky Chefs delivers roughly 8,000 meals a day to aircraft departing Helsinki Airport, according to a Finavia article (FI; year not stated; snippet). — [Finavia](https://finavia.fi/sv/node/4433)
- Star Food Finland (Vantaa) was the Finnair subsidiary Finncatering before LSG Sky Chefs bought it (FI). — [CAPA supplier profile](https://centreforaviation.com/data/profiles/suppliers/star-food-finland) (snippet)
- The company register gives Finnair Kitchen Oy's address as Tietotie 9, 01530 Vantaa, formerly named Finnair Catering (FI). — [Company register](https://companies.kimsalmi.com/en/finland/company/1633153-3) (snippet)
- **Special-meal cut-off (FI, current):** Finnair special meals "must be placed at the latest 24 hours before departure", via Manage booking or the Finnair Plus profile. On long-haul, Economy and Premium Economy have no nut-free special meal. The Business Class SPML contains no gluten, nuts, peanuts, eggs, milk, fish or crustaceans. On short-haul, special meals are Business Class only. Finnair "cannot guarantee an allergen-free" cabin. Customer service cannot pass allergy information to cabin crew, so the passenger must tell the crew at boarding. — [Finnair special diets](https://www.finnair.com/en/nordic-kitchen/special-diets)
- **Same 24 h rule elsewhere (US / HK, current):** Delta says special-meal requests made less than 24 h before the flight will not be on board in time. Cathay asks for 24 h for special meals and 72 h for other special accommodations. — [Delta SPML](https://pro.delta.com/content/agency/hk/en/products-and-services/in-flight/special-meal-requests--spml-.html); [Cathay Pacific](https://flights.cathaypacific.com/en_GB/offers/inflight-entertainment/special-meals-allergies.html)
- Japan Airlines lets passengers opt out of the main meal up to 25 h before departure, which cuts meal counts ahead of the final count (JP; snippet). — [Business Traveller](https://www.businesstraveller.com/news/airlines/airline-catering-inflight-appetite/) (fetch returned 403; snippet only)
- dnata says that because airlines accept special meals as late as 24 h before departure, caterers "need to think a step ahead". Its teams analyse each airline's passenger demographics to predict which special meals each location will need (UAE/global; snippet). — [Business Traveller](https://www.businesstraveller.com/news/airlines/airline-catering-inflight-appetite/)
- **Final-count reconciliation (US, 2001, historical):** if the final passenger count exceeds the meal order and the meal bank is empty, customer service, the kitchen and operations decide together whether extra meals are feasible. On domestic flights, meals were forecast from passenger load, with standard alternate meals kept for last-minute changes. — [JumpSeat News 2001](https://jumpseatnews.com/2001/7/new-meal-management-programs/)
- Caterers often work without knowing the final passenger load, so they over-produce to avoid shortages, which creates waste (global, IT-vendor blog). — [HCLTech](https://www.hcltech.com/de-de/blogs/the-role-of-advanced-technologies-in-streamlining-operations-across-the-in-flight-catering-sector)
- Higher load factors leave fewer buffer seats and push caterers to load closer to booked numbers (global market report; snippet). — [Scotts International](https://www.scotts-international.com/report_detail,t2165434?orderPDF=2)
- If a flight is disrupted before the food leaves the kitchen, it is held in a refrigerated area (global trade press). — [AviationPros](https://www.aviationpros.com/ground-handling/press-release/53095492/on-time-aircraft-catering-delivery-mastering-the-art-and-science-of-flight-ready-food)
- gategroup is investing in AI-enabled forecasting, predictive inventory and automated production lines. Its data flows link kitchen operations to airline schedules and aircraft provisioning (global; Toronto example; PAX 2025). — [PAX International, Sep 2025](https://www.pax-intl.com/passenger-services/catering/2025/09/03/gategroup-powers-future-of-inflight-catering-with-ai-and-intelligent-automation/)

### Inferences
- The volatile part of demand sits at the flight-kitchen level: final counts, special meals at T-24 h, and disruptions. Flight kitchens buffer this with finished-meal stock and standard alternates. That suggests their raw-ingredient orders to a wholesaler are fairly steady and forecast-driven. The exceptions are spikes from schedule changes, new routes, charter or seasonal peaks (summer, which matches the seasonal hiring), and specific inputs for allergen-free meals.
- For a wholesaler agent, the most useful airline-related capabilities are likely: exact specs and allergen data for SPML-grade inputs (for example certified gluten-free or milk-free), and fast fulfilment for summer and charter peaks. Second-guessing the final count is less useful.
- Helsinki has two big flight-kitchen buyers (Finnair Kitchen, LSG/Star Food), so this is a few high-value accounts rather than a broad segment.

### Gaps
- I found no public source on flight-kitchen raw-material purchasing in Finland: supplier names, wholesaler use, order cut-offs or delivery windows. Whether Valio Aimo supplies Finnair Kitchen or LSG Helsinki is unconfirmed.
- I found no published figure for when the final meal count is fixed (for example T-3 h or T-6 h) at gategroup, LSG, DO&CO or Finnair.
- The current Finnair Kitchen / LSG ownership and volume split was not verified.
- I found no incident data (for example allergen mis-loads) for Nordic carriers.

### Takeaway

It runs a fixed menu (restaurant cars, IC Bistro, pre-agreed group meal packages). ### Inferences

## 3. Cruise ships and Baltic ferries: port provisioning windows, last-minute orders, missing items

### Takeaway
Cruise provisioning is a pit-stop problem. Loading happens in a window of a few hours on turnaround day (for example about 500 pallets per Royal Caribbean turnaround; 4-hour loading at Norwegian). A truck that misses the cut-off means the ship sails without it, and the fix is a much more expensive emergency or at-sea delivery. Royal Caribbean reported 99.96% on-time supply (7–10 of about 5,000 containers a year missed; US, 2017). Helsinki itself is mostly a transit port: 101 calls and 175,000+ guests in 2025, with only 15 turnaround calls planned for 2026. The everyday Finnish volume is the Baltic ferries, which cook mostly in their own galleys, and Tallink runs its own logistics centre near Tallinn.

### Cited Findings
- **Cut-off and cost of a miss (US, 2017):** Hellmann's Jonathan Bales: if containers miss the 3 p.m. delivery, "that ship is gone". Goods that miss the loading window may need delivery at sea, "often at the vendor's expense". Royal Caribbean ships about 5,000 containers a year, misses about 7–10, and reports 99.96% on-time supply delivery. Fleet Services agents work around the clock and use local suppliers for emergency stock. When food runs short, galley staff often change the menu. — [Inbound Logistics, "Cruise Control", Jan 2017](https://www.inboundlogistics.com/?p=14055)
- **Weekly volume, one ship (US, 2017):** Oasis of the Seas is restocked weekly in Fort Lauderdale, including 1,899 lb coffee and 7,397 lb cheese. — [Inbound Logistics 2017](https://www.inboundlogistics.com/?p=14055)
- **Forecasting systems (UK/US, 2017):** Carnival UK plans centrally in Southampton. Each evening, ships send consumption data to a planning module. Planners review the forecasts (LLamasoft Optimiza), which then feed replenishment and generate orders for the next port. Norwegian orders per ship using algorithms based on passenger and crew counts and cruise length. Norwegian loads frozen first, then produce, dry goods, then technical items, in about 4 h including security checks. It consolidates at hubs in Miami, Barcelona and Hamburg. Hellmann consolidates vendor deliveries in a 200,000 sq ft centre near Port Everglades, sorted by destination ship and palletised by storage category. — [Inbound Logistics 2017](https://www.inboundlogistics.com/?p=14055)
- **Turnaround day (US, 2024/2026, enthusiast blog):** the window is a few morning and early-afternoon hours, with crew starting at 6:30 a.m. About 500 pallets of new inventory arrive by truck at the terminal. — [Royal Caribbean Blog 2024](https://royalcaribbeanblog.com/2024/04/16/turnaround-day-cruise); [Royal Caribbean Blog 2026](https://www.royalcaribbeanblog.com/2026/07/25/how-royal-caribbean-turns-cruise-ships-around)
- A former ship chandler turned cruise-line contractor (Alpha One) is known for urgent sourcing. Its COO says cruise lines order about two pallets of an item at a time, not restaurant-size case quantities (US, 2023; snippet). — [Cruise Industry News 2023](https://cruiseindustrynews.com/cruise-news/2023/05/alpha-one-from-ship-chandler-to-contracts-with-major-cruise-lines)
- Ship chandlers run warehouses with forklifts, racking and custom software, and keep ready stock to supply a vessel at any time (global; snippet). — [Freightcourse](https://www.freightcourse.com/?p=233)
- Cruise Critic forum posts (anecdotal) describe heavy provisioning days, when weeks of dry goods are loaded, alternating with light days carrying only essential perishables. — [Cruise Critic forum](https://boards.cruisecritic.com/topic/2657587-ship-resupply-question)
- **Helsinki cruise volumes (FI):** 2025 had 101 international cruise calls and 175,000+ guests (+26.4% year on year), with six first-time ships and six overnight stays. 2024 had about 139,000 passengers on 97 calls. Total 2025 passengers across scheduled ferry and cruise traffic was 9.6 m. — [Port of Helsinki 2025 release](https://www.portofhelsinki.fi/en/about-us/port-of-helsinki/port-of-helsinki/whats-new/cargo-and-passenger-traffic-volumes-in-the-port-of-helsinki-remained-stable-in-2025/); [Port of Helsinki 2024 release](https://www.portofhelsinki.fi/en/about-us/port-of-helsinki/whats-new/ship-travel-increased-moderately-in-helsinki-in-2024-cargo-traffic-volumes-the-same-as-the-previous-year/)
- 2026 season: 15 turnaround calls, 8 of them large ships carrying thousands of passengers, and more than 110 calls expected in total (FI). The monthly 2025 traffic tables show zero turnaround passengers, which conflicts with the "increasing turnaround" message; the classification is unclear. — [Port of Helsinki 2026 season](https://www.portofhelsinki.fi/en/about-us/port-of-helsinki/whats-new/international-cruise-season-begins-25-march-turnaround-calls-increase/); [Port of Helsinki June 2025 traffic PDF](https://www.portofhelsinki.fi/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/6-Traffic-in-the-Port-of-Helsinki-Ltd-June-2025.pdf)
- **Viking Line (FI/AX/SE, current):** most food is cooked in the ships' own galleys, and close to 70% of raw ingredients come from Finland, Sweden and Estonia. From April 2025, Swedish farm Bjärefågel is the main chicken supplier for the buffets. — [Moottori.fi](https://moottori.fi/uutinen/ruoka-hyva-syy-merimatkailla-ilman-ahkya/) (snippet)
- Valio is a freight customer of Viking Line: since May 2025 its products go Turku–Stockholm on Viking Glory/Grace via a biofuel "green corridor" (FI/SE, 2025). This is a logistics relationship, not evidence that Viking Line buys from Valio Aimo. — [Viking Line / Cision](https://news.cision.com/viking-line-abp/r/valio-s-products-now-transported-across-the-baltic-sea-with-90-per-cent-fewer-emissions,c4148329); [Port of Turku Aboard 2025](https://aboard.portofturku.fi/en/2025/08/valio-and-viking-lines-green-logistics-solutions-have-become-a-competitive-advantage-in-the-swedish-market/)
- **Tallink (EE/FI):** the Tallink Logistics Center near Tallinn distributes goods to the Tallink and Silja Line fleet as well as hotels and offices. The company called an owned centre "the most sustainable and quick solution". Tallink Silja's Menu Nordic changes twice a year. — [Moodie Davitt Report](https://moodiedavittreport.com/?p=374013) (snippet); [Moottori.fi](https://moottori.fi/uutinen/viiden-velhon-kattaus-tallink-siljan-uusi-menu-nordic-vie-kielen/) (snippet)
- A University of Turku blog covers food waste from a shipping-company perspective (Baltic; not fetched). — [UTU BRE](https://sites.utu.fi/bre/?p=7884)

### Inferences
- Baltic ferries run fixed daily or overnight round trips, so their port calls are short but very frequent. That is a different pattern from a cruise ship's weekly mega-load: many medium-sized orders against a hard sailing time, with menus that change twice a year. Tallink routes much of its supply through its own Tallinn logistics centre, which may limit Finnish wholesaler share. Viking Line's Finnish sourcing makes it a more plausible wholesaler customer. (Inference; not verified.)
- Helsinki cruise calls are mostly transit calls. Visiting ships are more likely to need top-up and emergency deliveries (fresh produce, dairy, specific missing SKUs), usually through a port agent or chandler, than full provisioning. With 15 turnaround calls in 2026, full turnaround provisioning is a small but growing niche. Each such call is large, specified in advance, and hard-deadline: pallet-level quantities, exact specs, and a delivery slot of a few hours.
- At cruise scale, missing the cut-off forces an at-sea delivery, often at the vendor's cost, or a menu change. That penalty is far larger than any unit-price difference. The 99.96% on-time benchmark shows what reliability these buyers expect.

### Gaps
- I found no data on Finnish ship chandlers serving Helsinki cruise calls, their order lead times, or their rush surcharges.
- I found no data on Viking Line, Tallink Silja, Finnlines or Eckerö purchasing processes: order cut-offs, delivery at the ship or terminal, use of Valio Aimo.
- I found no incident reports of Baltic ferries sailing with missing food items.

## 4. Remote and industrial sites: mines, megaprojects, Defence Forces, offshore

### Takeaway
Leijona Catering is the dominant Finnish case: Defence Forces caterer since 2012, about 70,000–80,000 portions a day, field catering that switches between garrison and exercise modes, and CGI Aromi software. Finnish mines and megaprojects employ around 1,000 or more people at peaks (Kittilä over 1,000 including contractors; OL3 annual outage about 1,000 contractor workers, half from abroad). I found no public detail on their catering providers. Offshore practice (UK/NO) is weekly resupply plus emergency runs, with a 10–14-day on-site buffer.

### Cited Findings
- **Leijona Catering (FI):** responsible for Defence Forces food supply since 2012, described as the Defence Forces' strategic partner for garrison and field catering (2025 Defence Forces slide). Exercise main meals are dry food with hot or cold water added (FI, Dec 2025). — [Ruokavirasto/Defence Forces seminar slides, Dec 2025](https://www.ruokavirasto.fi/globalassets/teemat/terveytta-edistava-ruokavalio/ravitsemussitoumus/vrnseminaari_sotilaanravitsemus_02dec25_jakoon.pdf) (snippet)
- Volume: "up to 80,000 portions a day" across the Defence Forces, the Prison Service and other public bodies (company employer page; snippet) — [Duunitori Leijona Catering](https://duunitori.fi/yritys/leijona-catering). Compare CGI's figure of "more than 70,000 meals per day in Finnish Armed Forces garrisons", and 6,000 meals a week at the Armoured Brigade in Parola. — [CGI case study](https://cgi.com/uk/en-gb/case-study/aromi/cgi-waste-manager-reduces-food-waste-for-finnish-armed-forces) (the figures differ in scope and probably date; CGI implementation 2016, case study dated 2020)
- Leijona uses CGI Waste Manager (part of the CGI Aromi suite), in place since 2016 and rolled out to all its cafeterias, to cut waste in preparation, serving and plate waste. — [CGI case study](https://cgi.com/uk/en-gb/case-study/aromi/cgi-waste-manager-reduces-food-waste-for-finnish-armed-forces)
- In exercises, Leijona usually handles rations, but over long distances local civilians are used. Yle reported Lapland village women feeding soldiers (FI; snippet). — [Yle](https://yle.fi/a/74-20216175)
- A field-lunch trial replaced the traditional hot field lunch with a lunch bag three days a week in five garrisons. After a study during the COVID period, field portion sizes were increased and vegetables and fruit added (FI; snippet; the exact Yle article was not verified). — [Yle](https://yle.fi/a/74-20041361)
- Academic theses on food waste in Jääkäriprikaati field catering and on the corporatisation of Defence Forces catering exist (FI; not fetched). — [Doria: field catering food waste](https://doria.fi/handle/10024/195053); [Doria: corporatisation](https://www.doria.fi/handle/10024/124525)
- **Kittilä mine (Agnico Eagle, FI):** Agnico Eagle Finland Oy employed 463 people in 2025. Over 1,000 people work at the mine, about half employed by contractors. Job ads list staff lunch as a benefit, but the caterer is not named. — [Duunitori Agnico Eagle Finland](https://duunitori.fi/yritys/agnico-eagle-finland) (snippet); [Wikipedia: Kittilä mine](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Kittil%C3%A4_mine) (snippet)
- Agnico Eagle is expanding in Central Lapland, with large gold-deposit deals and a possible doubling of Finnish gold output, which implies more people on site (FI, 2026 news). — [Yle](https://yle.fi/a/74-20221757) (snippet)
- **Olkiluoto 3 (FI):** each OL3 annual outage brings about 1,000 contractor workers on top of TVO staff, about half from abroad. One source mentions workers from over 80 countries during construction. — [MTV Uutiset](https://www.mtvuutiset.fi/artikkeli/olkiluoto-3-huoltoon-poissa-pelista-pari-kuukautta/9108490) (snippet); [Power Magazine](https://www.powermag.com/olkiluoto-unit-3-provides-carbon-free-nuclear-power-and-energy-security-for-finland/) (snippet)
- **Offshore (UK/NO):** North Sea clients get weekly and emergency supply runs, and platforms hold a rolling 10–14-day supply of dry, frozen and shelf-stable goods (supplier marketing). — [AVS Global Supply, offshore UK](https://www.avsglobalsupply.com/blog-detail/offshore-supply-united-kingdom); [AVS, role of offshore catering companies](https://www.avsglobalsupply.com/blog-detail/the-role-of-offshore-catering-companies) (snippets)
- A missed weather or vessel window can push offshore projects weeks off schedule (industry opinion, ASCO). — [Offshore Magazine](https://www.offshore-mag.com/business-briefs/blog/55387814/asco-opinion-why-logistics-has-become-a-critical-factor-in-offshore-energy-security)
- Statoil (now Equinor) tendered a dedicated contract for offshore provisions supply (NO, 2016). — [Oilfield Technology 2016](https://www.oilfieldtechnology.com/offshore-and-subsea/13092016/Statoil-awarding-contract-for-offshore-supply-of-provisions)
- Offshore supply-vessel planning models treat platform orders as arriving through the year, and these orders cannot be split (academic). — [Offshore supply planning in a rolling horizon](https://typeset.io/pdf/offshore-supply-planning-in-a-rolling-time-horizon-efz83ters2.pdf)

### Inferences
- Defence Forces exercises are the best Finnish match for "large, different every time, needed fast, exact". Headcounts change with call-up cohorts (intakes in January and July) and exercise schedules. Large exercises move thousands of people to remote areas for days. Leijona's scale (70–80k portions a day) and its existing Aromi system make it a high-value but probably contract-framed buyer. Fit for an AI agent depends on whether exercise-specific supply is bought from the wholesaler or from Leijona's own contracts. (Inference.)
- Mine expansions and plant outages (OL3, about 1,000 extra workers for weeks) cause step-changes in camp or canteen headcount over a known period. These look like project-based demand surges: big, planned weeks ahead, but with accuracy-critical specs (multinational workforce, likely varied diets and allergies). (Inference; the diet mix is not sourced.)
- Arctic and remote delivery adds route and lead-time limits (Lapland distances), which makes a correct first delivery more valuable than unit price, as with offshore.

### Gaps
- No caterer is named for Kittilä, other Lapland mines or the OL3 site canteens.
- I found no data on Hanhikivi 1 site catering (project cancelled in 2022).
- I found no per-exercise portion counts or lead times for Leijona exercise supply, or how much Leijona buys from wholesalers vs direct suppliers.

- I found no Finnish offshore or Arctic research-station catering data.

## 5. Systems and data flows: forecasting, EDI, ship-provisioning platforms, catering planning

### Takeaway
Buyers in this strand already plan by algorithm: Carnival UK runs nightly consumption-to-order forecasting, Norwegian uses passenger, crew and length-based models, gategroup uses AI forecasting, and Leijona uses Aromi. Ship supply runs on RFQ platforms such as ShipServ, which has about 260 ship owners/managers with 10,000+ vessels and 43,000–52,000 suppliers. An AI purchasing agent would plug into requisition-to-RFQ-to-PO flows rather than replace basic ordering.

### Cited Findings
- **ShipServ (global):** over 260 ship owners and managers operating over 10,000 vessels, versus 11,000 trading vessels on a Marcura page. Supplier count: 43,000 (third-party listing), 50,000 (Marcura) or 52,000+ (ShipServ homepage). Marcura claims 14 m transactions a year and USD 7 bn GMV; ShipServ claims USD 5 bn+ trade value. All are vendor figures, unverified and inconsistent with each other. — [ShipServ corporate](https://corporate.shipserv.com); [Marcura procurement](https://marcura.com/procurement); [ShipServ buyers](https://corporate.shipserv.com/buyer-solutions/facilitate-trade)
- ShipServ workflow: the buyer creates a requisition, converts it to an RFQ, and sends it to chosen suppliers. Suppliers respond on the platform or through their own system integration. — [ShipServ help: creating REQs, RFQs and POs](https://corporate.shipserv.com/knowledgebase/creating-reqs-rfqs-and-pos-in-shipserv-trade)
- Offshore and ship provisioning sequence: need, requisition, quotation, final order, delivery coordination (consultancy; no durations given). — [MCT Consultancy](https://mctconsultancy.com/?p=4230) (snippet)
- Carnival UK nightly forecasting (LLamasoft Optimiza) and Norwegian algorithmic per-ship ordering — see section 3. — [Inbound Logistics 2017](https://www.inboundlogistics.com/?p=14055)
- gategroup AI forecasting and predictive inventory (PAX, 2025) — see section 1. — [PAX International](https://www.pax-intl.com/passenger-services/catering/2025/09/03/gategroup-powers-future-of-inflight-catering-with-ai-and-intelligent-automation/)
- Leijona Catering runs CGI Aromi Waste Manager (FI, since 2016). — [CGI](https://cgi.com/uk/en-gb/case-study/aromi/cgi-waste-manager-reduces-food-waste-for-finnish-armed-forces)

### Inferences
- In this strand, the agent is valuable when it receives structured demand signals (consumption data, headcounts, PAX counts, Aromi menu plans) and returns orders already checked for spec, allergen, cut-off and delivery slot. These buyers already forecast. Their pain is executing late changes and exceptions against hard deadlines.
- Cruise lines and chandlers trade via RFQ platforms. Wholesaler integration with ShipServ-type flows could matter for Helsinki cruise and ferry calls. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- I found no named airline catering planning systems at Finnair Kitchen.
- I found no EDI or e-ordering details for Baltic ferries or VR, and no Valio Aimo integration details.

## 6. Exceptions: stock-outs, substitutions, food safety and allergen rules, incidents

### Takeaway
When something is missing, the documented responses are: change the menu (cruise), pay for an emergency or at-sea delivery (often the vendor pays), use local suppliers through 24/7 fleet agents, or keep standard alternate meals (airline). Allergen handling is a hard constraint with explicit disclaimers. Finnair will not guarantee allergen-free and cannot serve some severe multi-allergy passengers.

### Cited Findings
- Cruise: missed goods may need at-sea delivery "often at the vendor's expense". 24/7 Fleet Services agents source emergency stock locally. Galleys change menus when short. Kuehne + Nagel keeps a daily-updated "do not ship" list, and some regulations require details on how food was cooked, frozen or produced (US/global, 2017). — [Inbound Logistics 2017](https://www.inboundlogistics.com/?p=14055)
- Airline: Finnair may be unable to offer a suitable meal for multiple or very severe allergies. Meals may contain traces of nuts or peanuts, or come from facilities that handle them (FI, current). — [Finnair special diets](https://www.finnair.com/en/nordic-kitchen/special-diets)
- Airline: alternate standard meals cover last-minute count changes, and extra meals are negotiated when the meal bank runs out (US, 2001). — [JumpSeat News](https://jumpseatnews.com/2001/7/new-meal-management-programs/)
- Port provisioning guidance (Rotterdam) discourages last-minute requests and recommends pre-submitted lists, sometimes with pre-order discounts (NL; snippet). — [ShipUniverse](https://shipuniverse.com/?p=3548)

### Inferences
- Last-minute orders carry a price premium, and pre-orders sometimes get discounts. Buyers still place last-minute orders, which shows they will pay for speed when the deadline binds.
- An agent should treat allergen and spec matching as a hard constraint. Any substitution must be approved by the buyer, because a wrong allergen at altitude or at sea has no fallback.

### Gaps
- I found no documented Nordic incidents of allergen errors or missing provisions on ferries, flights or trains.
- I found no data on substitution acceptance rates.

## 7. Price sensitivity: missing a deadline vs unit price

### Takeaway
The evidence is qualitative but consistent. A missed cruise cut-off means the ship sails and the fix is an at-sea delivery, often paid by the vendor. Lines benchmark 99.96% on-time supply. Offshore misses can cost weeks. Buyers keep buffers (10–14 days offshore) and over-produce (airline) rather than risk running short. I found no direct quantification of price elasticity versus a deadline miss.

### Cited Findings
- "If a delivery to Walmart misses 5 p.m., Walmart will still be there the next day", but if containers miss the ship's 3 p.m. cut-off, the ship is gone (US, 2017). — [Inbound Logistics 2017](https://www.inboundlogistics.com/?p=14055)
- Royal Caribbean misses 7–10 of about 5,000 containers a year (99.96% on time) (US, 2017). — [Inbound Logistics 2017](https://www.inboundlogistics.com/?p=14055)
- Caterers over-produce meals because the final load is unknown, accepting waste to avoid shortages (global). — [HCLTech](https://www.hcltech.com/de-de/blogs/the-role-of-advanced-technologies-in-streamlining-operations-across-the-in-flight-catering-sector)
- Offshore platforms keep a 10–14-day buffer, and missed vessel or weather windows push schedules out by weeks (UK). — [AVS Global Supply](https://www.avsglobalsupply.com/blog-detail/the-role-of-offshore-catering-companies) (snippet); [Offshore Magazine](https://www.offshore-mag.com/business-briefs/blog/55387814/asco-opinion-why-logistics-has-become-a-critical-factor-in-offshore-energy-security)
- Alpha One built its cruise business on urgent sourcing (US, 2023). — [Cruise Industry News](https://cruiseindustrynews.com/cruise-news/2023/05/alpha-one-from-ship-chandler-to-contracts-with-major-cruise-lines) (snippet)

### Inferences
- Over-production, buffer stock and vendor-paid at-sea deliveries all show buyers paying to avoid shortage. So for transport and remote caterers, delivery reliability and correct specs are worth more than small unit-price savings. This is inferred from behaviour, not measured.
- Best Finnish segment candidates in this strand, by fit with "large, varies every time, fast, exact": (1) Defence Forces exercise and field supply through Leijona; (2) cruise turnaround and top-up calls in Helsinki (15 turnarounds in 2026, 110+ calls); (3) mine and plant-outage camp surges in Lapland and Olkiluoto; (4) Baltic ferry galleys, which order often against hard sailing times; (5) flight kitchens (concentrated, high-spec, but buffered internally); (6) VR rail (fixed menu, lowest fit).

### Gaps
- I found no sourced EUR figure for the cost of a missed provisioning cut-off, airline catering penalty clauses, or chandler rush premiums.
