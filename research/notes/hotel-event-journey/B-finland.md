# B. Finland lens: how a Finnish hotel gets ingredients for a large event

Research agent B, 14 Nov 2026. Method: WebSearch + WebFetch only. Tags: SOURCED (I fetched the page myself and read it), SOURCED-2 (only seen via a search-result summary, page not opened or fetch failed; treat as lower confidence), INFERRED, ASSERTED. Confidence H/M/L. Scope = Finland / chain / independent / named hotel.

Headline: the Finnish hotel terms I could read are very consistent on one thing: **the hotel's event "specification" (final headcount, special diets, meal times, menu choices) is due about 2 weeks before the event for groups of 50 or so, up to 3 weeks (VALO, 51-200 people), and changes after that are charged or re-priced.** Almost nothing public was found on how hotels turn that into wholesaler orders (Q3-Q7 are thin).

## 1. Claims by question

### Q1 Trigger and timeline

- **B1.** Large-event booking is a sales-led, written process: a group/event booking is made orally or in writing, becomes binding when the customer confirms and the hotel confirms in writing, and the hotel then asks for "details" by fixed deadlines. [SOURCED: VALO Hotel & Work general terms 2025, https://valo.fi/userassets/uploads/2025/10/VALO_Yleiset-toimitusehdot-2025-2.pdf — booking binding on written hotel confirmation; details due 7/14/21 days before arrival by group size] H, named chain/hotel (VALO Helsinki).
- **B2.** For S Group hotels the "event specification" (tilaisuuden tarkennus) is due at least 2 weeks before the group's arrival; it carries special diets and meal times. [SOURCED: https://www.sokoshotels.fi/en/terms-of-reservation/events — event specification due "at least two (2) weeks"; special diets and mealtimes go in it] H, chain (S Group hotels and restaurants).
- **B3.** Catering-style event providers in Finland ask to have catering and final head count confirmed 2 weeks before. [SOURCED: https://lawinsider.com/fi/contracts/4XS86tCjto4 — Royal Event Catering 2025 terms: confirm at least two weeks before] M. Scope: a catering company, NOT a hotel; used only as a comparator.
- **B4.** Customers book very early for peak days. S Group said booking for Christmas Eve restaurant tables accelerates about 100 days before; by early Dec 2024 it had nearly as many bookings as meals served the previous year. [SOURCED: https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/jouluaaton-valmis-poyta-ja-hotellijoulu-ovat-nouseva-trendi/ — 12 Dec 2024] M. Scope: S Group restaurants, Christmas Eve (not pikkujoulut, not event groups).
- **B5.** Cancellation schedules show how early big events are locked. Haikko (from 1 July 2026): for 150+ person-days, 100% can be released free until 120 days before; 14 days before the event the free portion has largely gone. [SOURCED: https://www.haikko.fi/fi/ehdot-ja-saannot/toimitusehdot-ryhmille] H, named hotel (Haikko Manor & Spa, Porvoo). VALO: 100% free until 120 days for 201-250 persons, 90 days for 101-200, 75 days for 61-100. [SOURCED: VALO terms, above] H.
- **B6.** When ingredients are ordered is NOT stated in any hotel document I found. The only anchor is wholesaler lead time: Kespro's Ravintolatukku (Helsinki region, then Lahti, Turku, Tampere) delivers 6/10/24 h after ordering; order before 09:00 arrives from 14:00 the same day; "for events, orders can be accepted until morning"; holiday and Sunday ordering possible. [SOURCED: https://www.kespro.com/ideat-ja-inspiraatiot/artikkelit/ravintolatukku-palvelee-ripeasti] M. Scope: Kespro, metro areas. Outside metros: order by 15:00 the day before for next-morning delivery in Jyväskylä, Kuopio, Oulu [SOURCED-2: Kespro article via search summary].
- **B7.** [INFERRED: from B2, B6, Valio Aimo "average 24 hours" delivery and its per-product closing times in GROUND_TRUTH 3/5] Order timing is probably in layers: (a) long-lead or special items ordered once the 2-week spec is in; (b) fresh/chilled top-up 1-3 days before; (c) emergency same-day top-up. Valio Aimo's equivalents are unverified. L.

### Q2 Head counts, dietary lists, billing, late change

Evidence table in section 2. Cross-cutting claims:

- **B8.** There is no single national rule; deadlines vary by hotel, by group size and by service: 3 business days for final headcount changes (Rantapuisto), 2 weeks for headcount+diets+menus (Rantapuisto, S Group, Royal Event Catering), 7/14/21 days by group size (VALO). [SOURCED: see table] H.
- **B9.** Headcount down: the hotel typically bills the confirmed number or a minimum. S Group: where meetings have no separate room rent, a minimum order amount is billed; any shortfall becomes room rent at the per-person catering price (worked example in the terms: 30 min, 25 attend, EUR 35 pp, 2 days = EUR 350). [SOURCED: https://www.sokoshotels.fi/en/terms-of-reservation/events] H, chain.
- **B10.** Headcount up: price follows actual numbers (S Group, Rantapuisto, Haikko). [SOURCED: those three term sets] H. Hotel may also move the event to a different room and renegotiate details and prices if numbers change substantially. [SOURCED: Rantapuisto https://www.rantapuisto.fi/kokous/ryhma-ja-kokousvarausten-ehdot/; VALO]
- **B11.** Late diet/detail changes: VALO says after the deadline the hotel may revise the price, charge separate room rent, change the room and renegotiate details. [SOURCED: VALO terms] H. Scandic: special requests such as diets should be given as early as possible; if too close the hotel may not be able to meet them. [SOURCED-2: Scandic T&Cs via search summary; fetch timed out] M.
- **B12.** Release of numbers is a sliding scale tied to days-out and group size, not a flat deadline. Haikko and Rantapuisto and S Group all publish tables (e.g. S Group 201-350 people: 100% charged inside 112 days, 50% inside 56, 25% inside 42, 10% inside 14 days; table layout was ambiguous in extraction so treat exact cells as M). [SOURCED: S Group terms, Haikko, Rantapuisto] M-H.
- **B13.** Royal Event Catering: if attendance falls more than 10% the provider may adjust unit prices; advance payment 50-100% must be in 7 days before the event. [SOURCED: lawinsider contract page] M, caterer not hotel.
- **B14.** VALO explicitly reserves the right to set different terms for hotel-run special events, "e.g. the hotel's own pikkujoulut". [SOURCED: VALO terms 7.3] H for that hotel. Meaning: pikkujoulut are sold as a separate product with their own terms; a generic table may not apply.
- **B15.** Wedding-specific hotel terms were NOT found. Haikko's wedding pages give price/headcount limits (Villa Haikko up to 230 people; buffet minimum 30) and mark dishes L (lactose-free), G (gluten-free), V (vegan) but state no deadline for diet reporting. [SOURCED-2: Haikko pages via search summary] L-M. Any wedding deadline claim would be [INFERRED] from the group terms above.

### Q8 Inside the manager's head (Finland-specific)

- **B39.** Finnish allergen rule: serving places must give allergen information for non-prepacked food (EU 1169/2011 Art 44; Finnish decree MMM 834/2014); lactose-free/gluten-free claims are voluntary labels but the restaurant is responsible that special-diet labels are correct and reliable (cross-contact example). [SOURCED-2: Ruokavirasto doc page via search summary; GROUND_TRUTH 6 confirms the legal base] M-H.
- **B40.** Kitchens are expected to hold allergen data per recipe; JAMIX imports allergen data from Synkka. [SOURCED: https://www.jamix.com/?p=14496] M. This is why dietary detail matters upstream of the wholesaler: product choice has to be traceable.
- **B41.** Lactose-free is mainstream in Finland: S Group said in 2015 that about 17% of all milk sold was lactose-free (35% of its Kotimaista own-brand milk) [SOURCED: https://yle.fi/aihe/a/20-122395, 26 Feb 2015, S Group figure, 10 years old]; Haikko's menu notation puts L (lactose-free) first among diet marks; Radisson Blu Royal Helsinki says its food is "primarily lactose-free" [SOURCED-2: search summary of raflaamo.fi pages]. No current national figure found. M, dated.
- **B42.** Pikkujoulu and December peaks: hotels sell fixed-date buffet pikkujoulut (e.g. Lapland Hotels Ounasvaara 27 Nov 2026, EUR 52 pp; separate cabin dinner EUR 68, min group 12) [SOURCED-2: a.storyblok.com PDF via search summary] M. Sokos Hotels' own tips page says venues sell out quickly [SOURCED-2]. I found no capacity or volume statistics (MaRa etc.). L.
- **B43.** Delivery constraints in Finland: Kespro's fastest rhythm is metro-only; regional towns get next-morning delivery against a 15:00 cut-off [SOURCED-2]. Valio Aimo delivers from Espoo and Kuopio warehouses (GROUND_TRUTH 5). [INFERRED] rural/resort hotels (Lapland, Koli, Vuokatti, Ruka are all in the search results) face longer lead times and fewer delivery days, so they order further ahead. L.
- **B44.** [INFERRED: B8, B11, B12] What a manager fears: (a) late headcount/diet change after the spec deadline when product is already ordered; (b) lactose-free/gluten-free volume short; (c) product substitution that alters allergen status; (d) peak-week stockouts; (e) billing exposure when numbers fall. Not sourced to an interview.

### Q9 What contradicts the brief's framing

- **B45.** 1,000 L of lactose-free milk for ONE hotel event looks implausible. Finnish per-capita drinking milk consumption is about 85 L per year (Luke, via Yle). [SOURCED: https://yle.fi/a/74-20227449] M-H, national. So 1,000 L is roughly 12 people's whole-year consumption. [INFERRED: arithmetic] For a 1,000-guest banquet, even at an assumed 0.2-0.3 L milk per guest in coffee/cooking/drinks [ASSERTED, not verified], total milk is 200-300 L, and lactose-free only a fraction (B41 suggests 15-35% in retail, dated). So a single-event order of 1,000 L lactose-free is more like a chain-wide, weekly or multi-property volume, or a deliberately large test number. Per-breakfast or per-guest milk data: NOT FOUND. L-M.
- **B46.** The data in GROUND_TRUTH 4.3 agrees in effect: only about 1,517 L of lactose-free milk with real stock exists across four products in the mock, so the brief's number is hard to meet from stock. [SOURCED: GROUND_TRUTH 4.3] H (the mock).
- **B47.** "By Monday" mirrors real practice only loosely: hotels would order against a 2-week spec for events (B2) and use wholesaler 24 h rhythm for top-ups (B6). A request made the Friday or Saturday before a Monday is a rush top-up, not the normal ordering moment. [INFERRED] M.
- **B48.** The brief frames a buying agent asking for a product by need. In Finnish hotels the hotel's own T&Cs push specification to the CLIENT 2-3 weeks out; the wholesaler-facing side is then a kitchen task driven by recipe, headcount and diet list. [INFERRED from B2, B8, B27] M.

## 4. The Finnish hotel decision-maker's mental checklist (head chef / F&B manager)

All [INFERRED] unless noted.
1. What exactly was sold: set menu or buffet, price per head, which minimum (S Group terms bill minimum order, B9)?
2. Is the headcount locked? Which deadline applies (14 days, 21 days, 3 business days)? What did the client already pay?
3. Diet list: how many lactose-free, gluten-free, vegan, allergy cases? Allergen data per dish must be right (B39); a wrong "lactose-free" label is the hotel's responsibility.
4. Are the recipes standard and up to date? What is the portion yield for this headcount?
5. What do I already have in stock, and what must I order? What is long-lead vs fresh?
6. Which wholesaler cut-off and delivery day applies at my location (rural vs metro, B43)?
7. If an item is unavailable, what substitute keeps the diet/allergen promise? Who may approve a substitute?
8. Do I have budget (food cost per head) and who signs for a spend above it?
9. If the client adds 15 guests two days before, can I still deliver (stock, staff, wholesaler cut-off)?
10. If the client cuts numbers, what is my exposure (billing rule) and can I return or reuse stock?

## 5. Questions only a hotel or Valio Aimo could answer

Hotel (head chef / F&B manager / sales):
1. At which point in the 14-21 day window do you actually place the first order, and which items are ordered in a second round?
2. Who builds the purchase list for a banquet and in what tool? Does JAMIX/Aromi or a spreadsheet drive it?
3. Do you order per event or from a standing weekly order with event top-ups?
4. Is purchasing central (chain agreement, assortment limited) or property-level? Which wholesaler(s) are contractual?
5. What size is the biggest event you served last pikkujoulu season, and what share of guests were lactose-free/gluten-free/vegan/allergic?
6. What happens when the client changes diets inside the deadline: do you accept, charge, or refuse?
7. Would you let a software agent place orders; up to what value; who approves substitutes?

Valio Aimo:
1. Typical lead time and cut-off per category for large event orders; peak-season (Nov-Dec) capacity rules?
2. Do hotels place event orders with event context (date, guests), or only product lines? Would that context help picking/substitution?
3. Real volume distribution: what is the largest single lactose-free milk order a hotel places, per order and per week?
4. Is Jamix/Aromi ordering used by any customer against Valio Aimo (EDI or file import)?
