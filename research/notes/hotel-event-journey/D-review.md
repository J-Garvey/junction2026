## 1. Verdict summary

1. The three reports mostly do not contradict each other; they answer at different scopes. The real conflicts are (a) 1,000 L demand vs 1,000 L as an order and (b) US 72 h guarantees vs Finnish 2-to-3-week specifications. Both are SCOPE-SPLITS, not errors.
2. For a Finnish hotel, the binding deadline is the **event specification (headcount, special diets, menu) at about 2 weeks** (S Group, Rantapuisto), or **7/14/21 days by group size** (VALO Hotel & Work). Some hotels also allow **final headcount changes up to 3 working days before** (Rantapuisto). I verified all three. [SOURCED]
3. Valio Aimo's public order model (24 h average, per-product closing time, carts for several weeks, edits until picking) means a hotel can place event lines when the specification lands and adjust them later. No public source says hotels actually do this. [SOURCED for the model; INFERRED for the behaviour]
4. **1,000 L of lactose-free milk is not one event's need.** Vendor breakfast guides give 0.1–0.25 L of milk per guest, so a 1,000-guest event uses roughly 100–250 L of milk in total. 1,000 L is closer to several weeks for one large hotel, or a week across a chain. Nothing public stops it being placed as one webshop order, but the mock has no single product with 1,000 L of real stock. [INFERRED from SOURCED inputs]
5. No structured event context (headcount, menu, event type) reaches Valio Aimo or Kespro in any public channel. Only order lines, a delivery date and, optionally, a phone or email heads-up do. **CONFIRMED** across all three reports.
6. **My Aimo "Minun reseptit" exists and is newer than the reports think:** an article dated 14 Jan 2026 calls it new. You paste in a recipe (A.I.mo helps), can change portion sizes and portion numbers, and send the recipe to the cart, which adds the smallest sales unit covering the need. The cart then carries ordinary lines, with no event attached. [SOURCED]
7. Substitution: the customer decides at both wholesalers, at different moments. Valio Aimo's webshop suggests substitutes at order time. Kespro Restaurant Wholesale's warehouse calls ahead during loading. What Valio Aimo does at picking is unpublished. [SOURCED + OPEN]
8. Still unresolved: which wholesaler(s) the big Finnish chains use and whether they buy centrally, where chain hotels keep recipes, and Valio Aimo's lead times for special, peak-season and VALO Tilauspalvelu orders. Only a hotel F&B person or Valio Aimo can settle these.
9. Corrections needed: B misreads the direction of the S Group cancellation table; B over-reads the VALO pikkujoulu clause; the name "VALO" means two unrelated things across B and C; C's "route 1,000 L via VALO Tilauspalvelu" is inference, not sourced.
10. Most US-sourced practice in A (72 h guarantee, 5% overset, punch-out/EDI) has **no Finnish evidence** and should not be put on a Finnish journey map without a hotel confirming it.

## 3. Disputes (ranked by how much they change the customer journey)

### D1. When do hotels order event ingredients, relative to headcount and diet deadlines and to wholesaler cut-offs?
- **A:** guarantee 72 h / 3 working days (A8, US/CA, snippets only); menu 2–4 weeks out (A3, consumer pages); ingredients ordered in the last 1–2 weeks, perishables in the last days (A5, INFERRED). Medium-low.
- **B:** Finnish specification at 2 weeks (S Group, Rantapuisto) or 7/14/21 days (VALO); last headcount change 3 working days (Rantapuisto); layered ordering of long-lead items at the spec, then fresh top-ups 1–3 days before (B7, INFERRED, L). Strong on deadlines, nothing on ordering.
- **C:** Valio Aimo carts can sit weeks ahead and be edited until picking; 24 h average delivery; per-product closing times; standing orders need 7 days to change (C1, C4, C5). Strong on the model, nothing on hotel behaviour.
- **Checked:** all three Finnish hotel T&Cs and the Valio Aimo delivery page (section 2 table). In the mock, Terminal items close 4–6 days before Monday delivery (GT 3). No source anywhere states when a hotel places its ingredient order.
- **Ruling: SCOPE-SPLIT.** 72 h is real in US/CA/IE (verified for Amber Springs). Finland verifiably runs a **two-step pattern**: specification (count + diets + menu) at **7–21 days by size**, with some hotels allowing final count changes up to **3 working days**. The two scopes share the "final small change a few days out" step. For a Finnish hotel ordering from Valio Aimo, the defensible sequence is: the order can exist from the specification date and remain editable until picking or closing time. The **actual ordering moment** is still **UNRESOLVED** [INFERRED]. To settle it, ask a Finnish hotel head chef when the first and last orders go in for a 200-guest event, and ask Valio Aimo how far ahead hotel order lines are typically dated.

### D2. Is 1,000 L of lactose-free milk plausible, and at what scope?
- **A (A37)** and **B (B45)**: implausible for one event; arithmetic from assumptions (A: coffee cups; B: 0.2–0.3 L per guest, ASSERTED).
- **C (C46)**: plausible as a webshop order because nothing public treats size differently; the mock cannot cover it from stock.
- **Checked:** two vendor guides from the same publisher give milk per breakfast guest as **100–150 ml** [SOURCED: https://kitchennmbrs.app/en/knowledge-base/breakfast-brunch-calculation/how-do-i-calculate-the-portion-per-person-for-a-breakfast-buffet] and **200–250 ml** [SOURCED: https://kitchennmbrs.app/en/knowledge-base/breakfast-brunch-calculation/how-do-i-calculate-the-amount-of-products-i-need-for-a-breakfast-buffet-for-30-people]. These are Dutch vendor blogs, low authority, but they agree with B's assumed range. National use is 85 L per person per year [SOURCED: Yle]. The lactose-free share is 17% of S Group milk in 2015 [SOURCED: Yle 2015]; no current figure was found. No Finnish chain publishes breakfast or milk volumes (searched).
- **Arithmetic [INFERRED]:** (a) 1,000-guest event at 0.1–0.25 L per guest = 100–250 L of milk of all kinds, so 1,000 L lactose-free is 4–10× even if every drop served were lactose-free. (b) One hotel serving ~400 breakfasts a day: 7 × 400 × 0.1–0.25 = 280–700 L a week, so 1,000 L ≈ 1.5–3.5 weeks for one large property. (c) A chain of 10–50 hotels: plausibly a week or less. The 400 breakfasts and the chain sizes are [ASSERTED] illustrations, not data.
- **Ruling: SCOPE-SPLIT.** As **one event's demand: OVERTURNED** (implausible by a factor of 4–10). As **chain-wide or multi-week property volume: plausible** (not verified). As **an order the webshop would accept: CONFIRMED by absence of any public size rule** (C46), with the mock unable to fill it from one product's stock (GT 4.3). A hotel F&B manager (weekly lactose-free litres) or Valio Aimo (largest single lactose-free line orders from hotel customers) could settle the real volume.

### D4. My Aimo "My Recipes": does it exist, what does it do, does it scale by portions?
- **B (B29):** exists; portion scaling not stated. **C (C12, C16):** exists; recipe to cart; margin; retention unknown. Neither had the detail.
- **Checked:** Valio Aimo article "Reseptisi ja katelaskenta Minun Aimossa", dated **14 Jan 2026** and describing the feature as new [SOURCED: https://valioaimo.fi/artikkelit/reseptisi-ja-katelaskenta-minun-aimossa]:

- **Ruling: CONFIRMED** (exists, recipe-to-cart, rounds up to whole sales units). **Scaling by number of portions: CONFIRMED with medium confidence.** "Annosten määrä" most naturally reads as the number of portions, but the article never says "multiply by guests". **UNRESOLVED:** whether a hotel would scale a banquet this way, and whether several recipes merge into one cart line per product. A webshop test account (the brief promises one) or Valio Aimo online sales (mentor Minni Ruhtinas) could settle it.
