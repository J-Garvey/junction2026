## Discrepancies

### A1-001 — HIGH — Rounded comparison prices corrupt whole-pack expectations

Owner: E3 oracle author. Second auditor: **required, unassigned by A1**.

At reviewed `corpus-v2.mjs` lines 94–100, pack size is the exact ratio
`catalogPrice.value / comparisonPrice.value`. Comparison prices are rounded
money values, not independent exact pack evidence. This creates both extra and
missing packs and therefore incorrect totals, stock checks and rounding questions.

Development reproduction: `aq2-ordinary-0064`, L002, SKU `464856` has manufacturer
`net_kg=1.6`, base unit ST, returned sales unit ST and `units.ST=1`. For 40 kg,
25 whole units cost 25 × EUR 25.15 = **EUR 628.75**. The frozen oracle divides
25.15 by rounded 15.72, infers 1.5998727735 kg, and expects **26 units / EUR 653.90**.
Other diet blockers on that particular line do not make its price arithmetic
valid. A development line without diet/cutoff blockers,
`aq2-long-0032` L052 SKU `463203`, also differs: 40 L requires 20 rather than the
frozen 21 units.

Full aggregate: 1,166 pack-count disagreements among 16,481 comparable lines.
Of these, **339** have manufacturer/base-unit support and comparison prices
consistent with that conversion to half a cent; **262** are in development.
The remaining discrepancies include inferred metadata or conflicting source
facts and require verification. Do not label every disagreement a proven error.

Acceptance: derive packs from verified base-unit content and the API sales unit;
leave disputed/inferred conversions unknown until independently verified.
Recompute quantities, totals, shared stock and rounding questions under a
versioned, independently approved expectation change. Preserve the original freeze.

### A1-002 — HIGH — State expectations cannot adjudicate complete conversations

Owner: E3 state-oracle author. Second auditor: **required, unassigned by A1**.

At reviewed lines 176–191, every noninitial `turns[].operation` is copied from
the scenario's family and every turn uses the original scope and
`confirmed:false`. There is no specified distinction between the scope bound to
a pending change and the currently selected scope. There are no exact expected
final basket quantities, preview state, order count, approval count or receipts.
Some invariants and a cancellation-specific unchanged flag exist, but do not
provide the positive expected transition for successful flows.

Development reproductions:

- `aq2-conversation-0003`: text `show Darragh` has expected scope `Conference`,
  as does the subsequent confirmation. Selected scope and pending binding are
  not independently represented.
- `aq2-conversation-0004`: a quantity replacement and subsequent `confirm change`
  are both labeled `correction`, `confirmed:false`. Original lines retain 5,000 g,
  a separate correction says 10,000 g, and no final state resolves the applied
  quantity versus a safe failed preview.

All 150 conversations mark all turns unconfirmed; 135 have obvious
confirm/cancel/show messages whose operation field is only a family label.
All 50 transport cases have no per-turn expectation array. These are schema and
coverage findings, not claims that every confirmation should succeed: catalog,
stock and policy failures must be evaluated first. Generic no-duplicate and
no-premature-mutation invariants alone could accept a permanently inert system.

Acceptance: independently specify initial seeded state and authority, actual turn
intent, current selection, pending binding, corrected requested quantities,
allowed failure/clarification, and exact final basket/order/approval effects.
Bind transport delivery IDs, replay and reconnect actions to those states.
Never derive expected outcomes from observed parser responses.

### A1-003 — MEDIUM — Exact-100 correction coverage is overstated

Owner: E3 corpus author; coordinator resolves campaign requirement interpretation.

Reviewed lines 149–173 add a correction only when `i % 3 === 0`. The 120
exact-100 requests therefore supply only 40 correction examples. Ten 20-position
long requests also cannot contain the intended second incompatible-repeat pair
at positions 26/27. Position links and the compatible repeat are present.

Acceptance: record explicitly whether the original combined requirement requires
at least 100 exact-100 requests containing corrections and both duplicate types.
Under that reading the current corpus fails. Add a separately versioned,
independently reviewed corpus if necessary; do not relabel the frozen 120 as 120
correction-bearing cases. Evaluate prose realism beyond the tag count.

### A1-004 — MEDIUM — Exact-SKU versus equivalent matching is not testable here

Owner: corpus author and coordinator/scoring owner.

Every one of 21,798 requested lines includes an explicit Aimo ID. The schema
has requested `productId` values but no per-line contract distinguishing an
exact mandatory SKU from an acceptable equivalent set or a question about
substitution. Random catalog/diet combinations mean just 2 of the 250 ordinary
cases have all lines clear under their own frozen oracle (this is not an actual
order-success rate). The broad unsupported-evidence cases are useful, but cannot
replace realistic ordinary matching and successful-state examples.

Acceptance: specify exact-choice preservation, equivalent acceptance and the
required question for substitution, with independently sourced evidence for
acceptable alternatives. Include realistic name-only hotel requests and viable
ordinary flows in a versioned corpus or explicitly narrow the campaign's coverage
claim. Continue treating unsupported certs, origin, delivery hours and batch-level
carbon as unverified; never manufacture evidence to improve coverage.

### A1-005 — MEDIUM — Per-line guard oracle omits date and price validity checks

Owner: E3 fixture/policy oracle author.

Synthetic reproduction against the reviewed exported `oracleForLine` supplies a
schedule with `availableDate:null` and a price whose `validTo` is before the fixed
clock. The result has `blocks:[]`, `unknowns:[]`, confirmed fixture stock, one
pack and a price. The code checks for a schedule object, not a valid delivery
date, and does not check the price validity interval or returned date versus the
requested date. The generic `recheckAtCommit:true` policy does not supply an
expected blocked result for these facts.

This is a controlled synthetic coverage probe, **not a claim that a frozen row
contains that pair of faults**. The existing cutoff/sentinel tests pass.

Acceptance: add independent expected guard behavior for absent/invalid/late
delivery dates and invalid prices, including validity at the relevant delivery
date/commit point. Preserve unknown facts and distinguish them from evidence of
confirmed availability. Author tests from the documented API and policy.
